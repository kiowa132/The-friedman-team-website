Marcus has been playing it patient all summer.



He's a first-time home buyer in Harford County, and every Maryland housing market update he's read since June has told him the same thing: more price cuts, more inventory, more room to negotiate. So he waited. He figured another few weeks would mean another few listings to choose from, and maybe a seller ready to come down on price. That strategy worked fine for most of August.



This week, it stopped working.



Statewide, new listings dropped to 1,064, down 14.2% from 1,240 the week before, the sharpest weekly drop in new Maryland housing inventory this report has tracked. At the same time, price reductions fell to 1,158 listings, down 5.1%, meaning fewer sellers felt pressure to cut. And pending contracts climbed to 374, up 4.8%, the clearest sign yet that Maryland buyers are moving again, not waiting on the sidelines.



Put those three together and you get a Maryland real estate market quietly tightening in the exact week most buyers assumed it was still loosening. Median sold price climbed to $440,000, up 4.8% from $420,000. Homes sold held essentially steady at 601. And Maryland mortgage rates eased for a second straight week, down to 6.65%, according to Freddie Mac, the first back-to-back weekly decline of the summer.



The inventory and price-cut window that buyers like Marcus had been counting on didn't stay open. It's not slammed shut, prices aren't spiking and days on market only ticked up slightly, but the easy negotiating leverage available a few weeks ago is thinner now.

\## What This Week's Numbers Really Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers



\*\*Maryland housing inventory tightened, quietly.\*\* New listings fell 14.2% week over week, the steepest drop in new supply this Maryland real estate market report has tracked, while price reductions fell 5.1% at the same time. Fewer new choices for buyers, and fewer sellers feeling pressure to cut, is exactly what a tightening market looks like, even without one dramatic headline number driving it.



\*\*Maryland mortgage rates eased for a second straight week.\*\* The 30-year fixed mortgage rate dropped to 6.65% this week, according to Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey, down from 6.67% the week before, marking the first back-to-back weekly decline of the summer. Freddie Mac's chief economist noted the dip is providing "modest relief for homebuyers," a notable shift in tone after two months of mostly one-directional rate movement.

\## Deep Dive: The Surprising Fact About Maryland Housing Supply This Week



Here's a number that puts this week's Maryland inventory data in perspective: Maryland's statewide months-of-supply currently sits at roughly \*\*2.8 months\*\*, according to recent third-party market data.



Months of supply measures how long it would take to sell every home currently on the market at the current sales pace. Under 3 months of supply is the classic threshold for a seller's market, buyers outnumber available homes enough that sellers hold real pricing power. Between 3 and 6 months is considered balanced. Above 6 months favors buyers. At 2.8 months, Maryland isn't just inside seller's-market territory, it's comfortably inside it, and that external supply reading lines up almost exactly with what this week's own numbers show: new listings down 14.2%, and price reductions down right alongside them.



One important caveat: that 2.8-month figure comes from a different dataset and methodology than this week's weekly Bright MLS pull used throughout this report, so it's a confirming signal rather than a direct match to this week's numbers. But both sources point the same direction: despite a summer of mortgage rate headlines and a few cooling weeks, Maryland's underlying supply-demand balance has stayed tighter than the week-to-week noise sometimes suggests.
