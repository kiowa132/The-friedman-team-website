Renee and Theo Alvarez started looking for a house in April.

By the middle of July they had been outbid three times, once by $40,000 over asking on a home they had walked through for eleven minutes. They decided to sit out the rest of the summer and come back when the market felt less frantic.

They came back the week after Labor Day, and there was a little more room than there had been in the spring. There were 2,016 new listings on the Maryland market that week. Homes were taking a median of 21 days to go under contract, up from 18 the week before. And 2,098 listings had cut their asking price.

The house they liked was in Frederick County, where the median sale this week landed near $484,000. It had been listed a little over three weeks and the sellers had already trimmed the price once. Theo wanted to come in low, but their lender's rate quote had just moved from 6.66% to 6.71%, and on a 30-year loan every eighth of a point is real money. So instead of a lowball they offered near the reduced list price, asked the sellers to cover part of the closing costs, and locked their rate that same afternoon. The offer was accepted in two days. There was no bidding war to win.

> Volume held its level this week. What moved was price and pace: a $10,000 dip in the statewide median, a few days added to the typical time to contract, and another uptick in mortgage rates.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of August 31 to September 6, 2026, the Maryland real estate market held steady with a mild softening for fall. Statewide closed sales held near 1,150 and new listings were essentially flat at 2,016, so transaction volume sat right where it was the week before. What actually changed was on the price and pace side: the median sold price fell 2.2% to $440,000, the median time to contract slowed from 18 days to 21, and the Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose five basis points to 6.71%, its second straight weekly increase. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 50 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Holding Steady). Harford County was the fastest-moving county in the state at a 25-day average, and Carroll County sellers still closed above their original list price. For buyers, this is a workable week. For sellers, it means the list price has to be right on day one.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### Home Sales Volume Held Steady

Closed sales across Maryland totaled 1,150 for the week, up 1.1% from 1,137 the prior week. New listings came in at 2,016, essentially unchanged from 2,019. Pending contracts, the best forward-looking read on demand, rose 5.7% to 518. Price reductions barely moved, at 2,098 versus 2,090.

Put together, those are the small, ordinary week-to-week wiggles you see in a market that is neither accelerating nor breaking down. Total closed sales volume for the week was $613,288,585 across the state.

### Maryland Home Prices Softened Modestly

The statewide median sold price slipped 2.2% to $440,000, giving back the $10,000 it had gained the week before. The median price on new listings coming to market fell by the same $10,000, to $419,900, which suggests sellers are reading the shift and pricing into it rather than testing high.

Even so, the softening is shallow. Homes that closed this week still sold for a median 99.0% of their original list price, and 100.0% of their most recent list price. In other words, the typical Maryland seller who priced correctly at launch is still getting essentially full ask. The price weakness in the data is concentrated in listings that started too high and had to chase the market down.

### Homes Are Taking Slightly Longer to Sell

The median time from listing to signed contract for closed sales moved from 18 days to 21 days. That is still fast by historical standards, but the direction is worth noting: buyers have picked up a little breathing room to schedule a second showing, order an inspection, and negotiate terms instead of firing off an offer sight unseen.

### Mortgage Rates Rose for a Second Straight Week

The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate averaged 6.71% for the week, up five basis points from 6.66%. It is the second consecutive weekly increase after the early-August decline. For a buyer financing a $440,000 purchase with 20% down, moving from 6.66% to 6.71% adds roughly $11 to the monthly principal-and-interest payment, and about $4,000 over the life of a 30-year loan. Small on its own, but it is trending the wrong way for buyers, which is why the rate-lock timing conversation matters right now.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of August 31 to September 6, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market with a slight lean toward buyers**. The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 50 out of 100, dead center on the scale.

Here is how the leverage actually breaks down this week:

- **Buyers gained a little ground.** There are more active listings to choose from, homes are sitting a few days longer, and 2,098 listings statewide have already cut their price, which gives a prepared buyer real room to negotiate on price and terms.
- **Sellers held most of their position.** Closed homes still realized a median 99.0% of original list price. Well-priced listings are still going under contract in about three weeks near full ask. The homes struggling are the ones that launched above the comps.
- **The wildcard is the mortgage rate.** At 6.71% and rising, affordability is tightening at the margin, which caps how far buyer leverage can run before it starts pricing some buyers out entirely.

If you are buying, this is a week to be in the market with financing locked. If you are selling, it is a week where the list price has to be defensible against recent comparable sales from day one.

## Mortgage Rates in Maryland: What Buyers Need to Know

The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed rate averaged 6.71% for the week of September 3, 2026, up five basis points and rising for the second straight week. The 15-year fixed averaged around 6.04%.

For Maryland buyers, a few practical points:

- **The trend matters more than the level.** A single five-basis-point move is minor. Two consecutive weekly increases is a pattern worth respecting. If you are within 60 days of closing and comfortable with the payment, locking now protects you from further increases.
- **Payment math on a typical purchase.** On a $440,000 home with 20% down ($352,000 financed), the jump from 6.66% to 6.71% adds roughly $11 per month and about $4,000 over 30 years. Over the full swing from the early-August lows, the difference is larger.
- **Rate buydowns and seller concessions are back on the table.** With 2,098 price reductions statewide and homes sitting a few days longer, more Maryland sellers are willing to contribute toward closing costs or a temporary rate buydown. That is a negotiation lever a good buyer's agent will push on right now.
- **Maryland-specific programs still apply.** First-time buyers should check Maryland Mortgage Program options and county-level down payment assistance, which can meaningfully change the effective cost of financing regardless of where the headline rate sits.

## Deep Dive: The 99% Number

The single most revealing figure in this week's data is **99.0%**, the median ratio of sold price to **original** list price across every Maryland home that closed during the week. Not the most recent list price. The very first one.

It matters because of everything happening around it. There were 2,098 price reductions statewide this week. The median sold price fell $10,000. Homes are taking longer to go under contract. By every one of those measures, it looks like a market where sellers are losing ground. And yet the typical closed sale still landed within one percent of the number the seller asked on the day the home hit the market.

The reconciliation is straightforward. The price weakness in the data is concentrated in the listings that were priced too high at launch and had to chase the market down with repeated cuts. The homes that were priced correctly from day one are still selling at, or very near, full ask. In a softening market, the gap between those two outcomes widens, and this week it was wide. The average sold-to-original ratio was 97.6%, pulled below the median by a smaller number of deeply discounted sales.

The takeaway for sellers: the market is not punishing you for selling in September. It is punishing overpricing, and it is doing it faster than it did three months ago.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Maryland Crab and Corn Chowder

The first cool nights of September, the last of the local sweet corn, and crab still running in the Bay. There is exactly one week a year this dish is perfectly in season, and this is it.

**Serves 4 to 6**

- 4 strips bacon, chopped
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 4 cups seafood or chicken stock
- 2 medium Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and diced
- 3 ears fresh corn, kernels cut off (about 2 cups; frozen works off-season)
- 1 cup heavy cream
- 1 lb Maryland lump crab meat, picked over for shell
- 1 tsp Old Bay, plus more to taste
- Salt and black pepper
- Chopped chives or parsley, to finish

1. In a heavy pot, cook the bacon over medium heat until crisp. Remove with a slotted spoon and set aside, leaving the fat in the pot.
2. Add the onion and celery and cook until soft, about 5 minutes. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute more.
3. Whisk in the stock a little at a time so it stays smooth. Add the potatoes, bring to a simmer, and cook until tender, about 12 minutes.
4. Add the corn and cream and simmer 5 minutes.
5. Gently fold in the crab and Old Bay and warm through, about 2 minutes. Do not let it boil hard or the crab toughens.
6. Season with salt, pepper, and more Old Bay to taste. Serve topped with the crispy bacon and chives.

Crusty bread and a green salad make it dinner. It reheats well; add the crab fresh to each reheated bowl if you can.

The homes seeing real price cuts are the ones that were overpriced at launch.
