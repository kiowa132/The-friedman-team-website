Picture a seller named Ron. His Baltimore County home is clean, priced well, and freshly painted, and the listing photos look fine until the living room, where the light rakes across a heavy textured ceiling and every bump throws a shadow. A buyer who loves the layout stops at the door and says, "I would have to redo all these ceilings."

That reaction is why sellers ask about popcorn ceilings. The good news is that removing them is often one of the most affordable ways to make a home look updated. The catch is that in an older home you cannot just start scraping.

**Quick answer:** Most homeowners spend roughly $900 to $3,100 to have popcorn ceilings professionally removed, and $100 to $500 per room for a careful DIY job. If your home was built before about 1990, test for asbestos before anyone touches the ceiling. Removal makes rooms look brighter and more modern in person and in photos, and it is worth considering when the ceilings are dated, but it is not a must-do for every home.

## Do You Need to Test for Asbestos First?

Yes, if your home is older, and this is not the step to skip.

Asbestos was banned from spray-applied ceiling texture in 1978, but manufacturers were allowed to use up existing stock afterward, so homes built into the early or mid 1980s can still have it. Testing is worth doing for anything built before about 1990.

Untouched, the material is generally not the concern. It becomes hazardous when it is disturbed and the fibers get into the air. Here is how the process works:

1. Hire a licensed professional to take a sample and send it to a lab. In Maryland, asbestos inspectors must be accredited by the state, and the testing typically costs a few hundred dollars.
2. If the result is positive, removal must be done by a licensed asbestos abatement contractor. That is a different and more expensive job than standard removal.
3. If the result is negative, you can move on to normal removal methods.

Do this before you list, not during a contract. A positive result you know about is a plan. A positive result a buyer's inspector discovers is a negotiation.

## Can You Remove a Popcorn Ceiling Yourself?

If the test is negative, a DIY scrape is manageable for a handy homeowner. The job takes one to three full days per room and it is messy.

**What you need:** plastic sheeting, a pump sprayer, a wide drywall knife, joint compound, ceiling-rated primer, flat ceiling paint, safety glasses, an N95 mask, and a ladder or scaffold.

**The process:**

1. Clear the room and cover the floors and walls completely with plastic.
2. Wet a small test section and see whether the texture scrapes off cleanly. Painted popcorn is harder to remove.
3. Mist and scrape in sections, about three feet square at a time.
4. Skim coat the ceiling to smooth out gouges and marks.
5. Sand, prime, and paint.

Hire a professional instead if the home is older and untested, the ceilings are high or vaulted, there is water damage or sagging, or you need a flawless, photo-ready finish before a listing goes live.

## What Are the Alternatives to Scraping?

- **Drywall overlay.** New drywall goes over the existing texture, at roughly $1.50 to $3.00 per square foot. It hides the texture completely, but it lowers the ceiling slightly and is harder to DIY. If the ceiling tests positive for asbestos, ask a licensed professional whether covering is an option.
- **Ceiling panels or shiplap.** A fast project at about $1,000 to $2,000 or more, and it adds a design feature. It suits some architectural styles better than others.
- **Light knockdown texture.** A middle ground that looks less dated than heavy popcorn without a fully smooth finish.

## Does Removing a Popcorn Ceiling Help Your Home Sell?

It helps how the home looks, and that is usually where it pays. A flat ceiling reflects light evenly, so rooms look brighter and larger. Listing photos come out cleaner, because there are no shadows across the ceiling. And buyers are less likely to ask for a credit to redo the ceilings themselves. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does popcorn ceiling removal cost?**

Professionally, roughly $300 to $1,000 for one room and $1,500 to $5,000 for a whole house, with most national estimates for a typical home landing between about $900 and $3,100. DIY is often $100 to $500 per room.

**Does my popcorn ceiling have asbestos?**

You cannot tell by looking. Homes built before about 1990 could, so have a sample tested by a licensed professional before disturbing the ceiling.

**Is it safe to scrape a popcorn ceiling myself?**

Only after a negative asbestos test. If the test is positive, a licensed abatement contractor has to do the work.

**How long does it take to remove a popcorn ceiling?**

A DIY job takes about one to three days per room, including drying, skim coating, priming, and painting. Professionals are usually faster.

**Should I remove popcorn ceilings before selling my house?**

Consider it if the ceilings are heavy and dated and the rest of the home is in good shape. It improves brightness and photos and can head off buyer objections, but there is no guaranteed return, so get your agent's opinion first.
