The Seller Who Almost Waited Too Long

Last week, a homeowner told me she was going to “wait until fall, when the market’s better.”



Here’s what I didn’t get to tell her in time: fall isn’t coming to save her. Right now, Baltimore County is sitting in a window most sellers don’t realize is closing inventory is still tight enough that well-priced homes are getting real attention, but it’s loosening every month. The buyers who are out there right now are motivated, pre-approved, and tired of losing out. The buyers who’ll be out there in October are pickier, because they’ll have more to choose from.



That’s the part nobody tells sellers: the best time to sell isn’t when the market feels perfect. It’s right before everyone else figures out it’s still good.



Every month a seller waits “for a better moment,” two things happen more competing inventory hits the market, and the pool of highly motivated buyers thins out. Days on market are creeping up slightly right now (38 days vs. the low-30s a few weeks ago), and that’s exactly the kind of quiet shift that turns into a real slowdown if sellers wait for “proof” before acting. By the time the data confirms it, the advantage is already gone.



If you’re sitting on a property and telling yourself you’ll list “when things settle down” things are about as settled as they’re going to get for a while. The sellers winning right now aren’t the ones with the lowest price. They’re the ones who moved while the window was still open.

Stop Waiting for the Rate Fairy

Every week, someone tells me they’re “waiting for rates to drop” before they buy. This week is the perfect example of why that’s the wrong strategy.



Rates fell five basis points this week. On a $400,000 loan, that’s a $12-a-month difference. Less than a Netflix subscription.



Meanwhile, that same buyer who waited for rates to move watched two other buyers make offers on the home they liked.



The real lesson: rates are not why people are buying or not buying right now. Confidence is. Inventory is. Timing your life around basis points is a losing game timing it around the right house at the right moment isn’t.



Not Every Deal Looks Like a Deal

A property just hit the market in Frostburg for $14,900. No, that’s not a typo.



It’s the kind of listing that makes people scroll past, assuming it’s a scam, or stop and wonder what’s really going on. In all likelihood, it’s a teardown or a land-value play the kind of property that scares off 95% of buyers and quietly makes the other 5% a lot of money, if they know what they’re looking at.



The takeaway for investors and house hackers: the most interesting opportunities in this market aren’t always the ones that look polished on the surface. Sometimes they’re the ones nobody else will touch.



Why the Top of the Market Matters to Everyone

Most Baltimore County buyers and sellers will never get near a $19.9 million estate in Chesapeake City. So why mention it?



Because the luxury tier sets the tone for confidence across the entire market. This particular property has sat active for 141 days and was just relisted a story about patience and pricing at the very top. Even at the highest end, the market rewards realistic pricing over wishful thinking. The same lesson applies whether you’re selling a $300,000 rowhome or a waterfront estate.

The Surprising Fact

On a $400,000 loan, this week’s rate decline to 6.47% saves a typical buyer only about $12 per month. Rate relief alone isn’t what’s driving the recent uptick in pending activity confidence and inventory dynamics are doing far more of the work than the rate itself.



Bottom line: Rates ticked down, but that’s not the headline. Baltimore County is holding a steady, proportional share of Maryland’s market. Days on market are creeping up a signal to watch, not panic over. And the real opportunities this week, as always, are in the details: a distressed lot in Frostburg, a luxury estate testing patience in Chesapeake City, and buyers everywhere quietly recalibrating what “normal” looks like.
