Danielle had been told to wait until after Labor Day.

She's a seller near Rockville, in Montgomery County, sitting on a
townhome she'd been debating listing since June. Every conversation with
her agent all summer landed on the same advice: hold off, let the
late-summer lull pass, wait for the "real" Maryland fall market to show
up in September.

Then last week's edition of this report crossed her feed — the one
flagging that new Maryland listings had dropped 14% and price cuts had
eased right alongside them. She read it, decided that was her signal, and
listed Tuesday morning.

She was not alone. Not even close.

> New listings and closings both nearly doubled statewide in the same
> seven-day window — the sharpest simultaneous supply-and-demand jump
> this report has tracked since it started.

**Quick answer:** Maryland's housing market for the week of August 24–30,
2026 isn't tilted clearly toward buyers or sellers — it flooded with
volume on both sides at once. Statewide new listings jumped 89.8% to
2,019 and closed sales jumped 89.2% to 1,137, both the largest single-week
moves this report has ever recorded. Median sold price rose to $450,000
(+2.3%), and homes that closed this week sold at a median 99.3% of their
*original* asking price. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 58 out
of 100 — Balanced Market, Surging Activity — up only modestly from 55
last week, because its four components pulled in different directions
instead of moving together.

By Sunday night, roughly 2,019 other Maryland sellers had made almost the
exact same call Danielle did, in the same seven days. Closings nearly
doubled right alongside the new supply, which is the part that actually
stands out — a market can absorb a flood of new inventory slowly, or it
can absorb it fast, and this week Maryland absorbed it fast. Danielle
watched her own showing requests come in that first week worried her
listing would disappear into the noise. It didn't: priced honestly to the
comps, it drew three offers and went under contract within days, right in
line with the statewide pace, where new listings moved to contract in a
median of just four days this week.

## What This Week's Numbers Really Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

**The market flooded back open, all at once.** New listings jumped 89.8%
and closings jumped 89.2% in the same week — both the sharpest weekly
moves this report has tracked, in either direction. That kind of
simultaneous supply-and-demand surge, right at the turn into fall, reads
less like a shift toward buyers or sellers and more like pent-up
decisions on both sides of the Maryland market landing in the same seven
days.

**Pricing power held, but reductions surged too.** Median sold price rose
again to $450,000 (+2.3%), and closed homes realized a median 99.3% of
their original list price — not their most recent list price, their
*original* one, meaning most of the real negotiating happened through
smart initial pricing rather than markdowns after the fact. But price
reductions jumped 80.5% statewide — the flip side of this much new
inventory hitting at once, and partly proportional to the volume surge
rather than a sign Maryland sellers are suddenly overreaching on price.

Frederick Counties) on pure pricing power: closed homes sold at a median

**Bottom line:** Montgomery shows that Maryland's biggest markets can
carry real volume without losing pace, while Carroll proves its
multi-week speed streak wasn't a fluke — it got faster even as the rest
of the state flooded with new inventory.

## The Friedman Signal™: Surging

This week isn't a story about which side of the Maryland housing market
is winning — it's a story about volume. New listings, closings, pending
contracts, and price reductions all jumped by the largest single-week
margins this report has tracked, several of them nearly doubling. That's
consistent with a seasonal turn — sellers who'd been sitting on listings
through late summer moved at once, and buyers who'd been waiting
responded in kind. Watch next week closely: if volume holds anywhere near
this level, late August marks the real start of Maryland's fall market.
If it snaps back down, this week was a one-time release valve, not a new
normal.

## Deep Dive: The Surprising Fact About Maryland Housing This Week

Add up every home that closed in Maryland this week and you get
**$606,704,645** in total sales volume across 1,137 transactions — nearly
double last week's closed volume, in step with the closings count itself.

Here's the flip side: **432 listings** statewide were withdrawn or
canceled this week without ever selling (220 withdrawn, 212 canceled) —
meaning for every 10 homes that successfully closed, roughly 4 others
quietly fell out of the pipeline entirely, never reaching a buyer. The
market's most visible number is always the one that sells; the ones that
don't rarely make headlines, and this week there were a lot of them.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Smoked Cream Cheese, Two Ways

<img src="/images/uploads/maryland-real-estate-market-report-week-of-august-24-30-2026-recipe.png" alt="A block of smoked cream cheese topped with fresh rosemary and a torched golden crust, served with crackers" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;" />

Labor Day weekend means one more cookout, and this is the easiest thing
I've put on the smoker all summer. We did two flavors — one savory, one
sweet and spicy — and both blocks were gone before the second round of
guests showed up.

**The method** (works for either flavor, serves 6–8 as an appetizer)
1. Preheat your smoker to 225°F. Any wood works — applewood for the ranch
   & garlic, cherry for the brown sugar & paprika.
2. Score the top of a cold 8 oz block of cream cheese in a crosshatch
   pattern, then coat it all over with a little olive oil so the
   seasoning sticks.
3. Coat generously with your topping of choice (below), pressing gently
   so it adheres.
4. Smoke for about 2 hours, until it's warmed through, slightly golden on
   top, and soft enough to spread.
5. Rest 5–10 minutes, then serve warm with crackers or toasted baguette.

**Ranch & Garlic** — 1 packet (or 2 tbsp) dry ranch seasoning + 1 tsp
garlic powder, topped with chopped chives after smoking.

**Brown Sugar & Paprika** — 3 tbsp brown sugar + 1 tsp smoked paprika + a
pinch of cayenne if you want heat, drizzled with honey right off the
smoker.

Zero skill required, disappears in minutes — possibly the only easy thing
about the Maryland market right now.
