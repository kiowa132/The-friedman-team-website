Maryland home sales rebounded sharply this week, and mortgage rates finally gave buyers and sellers a break after six straight weeks of increases. Here's the full county-by-county breakdown of what happened in the Maryland real estate market between August 10 and August 16, 2026.

> "It can process enormous amounts of data. It cannot see a house."

A seller I've been working with, I'll call her Renee, called me right after last week's report went out, the one showing a 32% drop in statewide closings. She wanted to pull her Owings, Maryland home off her "maybe list" entirely and wait until spring. She'd read the word "cooling" and heard "don't list now." I told her that report also showed the underlying buyer pipeline was healthy, pending contracts had barely moved, and that a one-week correction isn't the same thing as a market downturn. She wasn't fully convinced. She wanted to see the data prove itself for another week before trusting it.

This week proved it.

Statewide closings jumped back up to 609 homes, up 14.9% from 530 the week before. The median sold price climbed to $420,000, up 3.7%. And for the first time in six weeks, Maryland mortgage rates actually eased - the 30-year fixed dropped to 6.67%, down from 6.69%, according to Freddie Mac, snapping the longest streak of weekly increases this report has tracked all summer.

Here's the detail that mattered most for Renee specifically: her own county, Calvert, was the fastest-moving housing market in the entire state this week - a 22-day average days on market with 26 closings, real transaction volume behind a genuinely quick pace. That's not a coincidence, and for a Maryland seller sitting on the fence, it's exactly the kind of local signal that should carry weight.

Mortgage rates aren't dropping fast, and this week's small decline won't save anyone thousands of dollars overnight. But a Maryland housing market that's selling more homes, at higher prices, in fewer days, with borrowing costs finally moving the right direction, isn't a market to wait out. It's one to list into.

## What This Week's Numbers Really Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

**The rebound held, and then some.** Closings jumped 14.9% week over week to 609, continuing to confirm that last week's steep pullback was a temporary correction rather than the start of a longer slide. Median sold price climbed alongside it, up 3.7% to $420,000, giving Maryland sellers real, current evidence that pricing power hasn't disappeared.

**Mortgage rates finally took a breath.** The 30-year fixed mortgage rate eased to 6.67% this week, according to Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey, down from 6.69% the week before - the first weekly decline after six consecutive increases. Freddie Mac's chief economist described rates as "relatively stable" this week, a notable change in tone after a summer defined by one increase after another.

## Deep Dive: The Fun Fact About Maryland Home Sales This Week

Add up every home that closed in Maryland this week and you get **$291,497,384** in total sales volume, across 609 transactions. That's enough dollar volume to buy this week's priciest Maryland listing - the $4.6 million Annapolis waterfront estate - more than 63 times over.

Here's the flip side, though: 344 Maryland listings statewide were withdrawn, expired, or canceled this week without ever selling. That means for every 10 homes that successfully closed, roughly 5 to 6 others quietly fell out of the pipeline entirely. The market's most visible number is always the one home that sells. The listings that don't rarely make headlines, but this week, they very nearly kept pace with the ones that did - a reminder that behind every closing statistic in this report, there's a comparable volume of Maryland listings that didn't make it to the finish line.
