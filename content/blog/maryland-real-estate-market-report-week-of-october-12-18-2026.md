The text from Jordan came in at 6:12 on Monday morning. "Rate is still 7.40. Do we keep waiting?"

He and Casey had been waiting since August. Every Thursday they checked the Freddie Mac number, and every Thursday for seven weeks it had gone up. This week it finally held. Their agent, Marcus, called instead of texting back.

"The rate did not move, and that is useful, because now we can stop guessing about it," Marcus said. "Here is what did move. Maryland had 1,884 price reductions last week. In Baltimore County alone it was 213, against 95 closings. Sellers are cutting, and buyers who ask for the right things are getting them."

He had a listing in mind in Towson, a three-bedroom that had dropped $15,000 after eleven days on the market. Marcus did not suggest a lowball offer. He suggested an offer near the new list price with a seller credit of $9,000 toward closing costs, which on their loan would buy the rate down more than the price cut would have saved them each month.

Casey asked the question that mattered. "What if they say no to the credit?" Marcus had the answer ready: "Then we know by Wednesday, and there are two more reduced homes in the same zip code to see this weekend."

The sellers countered Tuesday night, met in the middle at $6,500, and accepted Wednesday afternoon. Jordan sent Marcus a photo of the signed page with one line under it: "Glad we stopped waiting for the rate to do the work."

> When sellers are cutting 4.6 times for every contract that goes pending, the buyer who asks for the right concession is the one who gets the house.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of October 12 to 18, 2026, the Maryland mortgage rate held while sellers kept cutting prices. The median sold price was $439,000, up 0.7% from $436,000 on a like-for-like re-pull. Closed sales reached an early count of 802, up 5.1% from last week's first count of 763, and new listings were 1,982, which is 2.47 new listings for every sale, down from 2.79 last week. Price reductions rose 2.6% to 1,884, or 4.60 for every contract that went pending, while pending contracts fell 5.3% to 410. The median time from listing to signed contract for homes that closed was 22 days, against 23 last week. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate held at 7.40%, the first week without an increase after seven straight rises. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 43 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Finding Its Footing), up one point. Howard County was the fastest-moving large county at a 23-day average, while Baltimore City and Prince George's County carried the most new supply relative to closings. For buyers, a reduced listing plus a seller concession is doing more work than waiting on the rate. For sellers, price for this week's buyers.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### The Rate Finally Held

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 7.40%, unchanged from last week, the first week without an increase after seven straight rises. On a $400,000 loan the payment is about $199 a month higher than at the 6.66% low this report tracked in late August, but it did not add another dollar this week.

### Closings Ticked Up on an Early Count

802 Maryland homes have closed so far this week, up 5.1% from last week's first count of 763 taken at the same point. Last week's count has since climbed to 922 as late closings posted, so expect this one to drift up too. Total closed volume so far is about $421.1 million.

### Price Held

The median sold price was $439,000, up 0.7% from last week's $436,000 on a like-for-like re-pull. Homes that closed sold for a median 98.7% of their original list price and 100.0% of final list price.

### Sellers Kept Cutting

Price reductions rose 2.6% to 1,884, against 1,836 last week. That is 4.60 price cuts for every contract that went pending, the second highest of the seven weeks this report has tracked, behind only the 5.23 in late September.

### Contracts Slipped

Pending contracts fell 5.3% to 410, from 433. Pending is the forward-looking number, and it has now moved between 408 and 445 for four weeks without breaking out in either direction.

### Speed Held Steady

Median days to contract was 22, against 23 on last week's re-count. The median home that closed this week took 20 days on market, and new listings came in at 1,982, down 6.8% from 2,126, or 2.47 new listings for every sale, down from 2.79 on first counts last week.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of October 12 to 18, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market finding its footing.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 43 out of 100, up one point from last week's reading of 42.

- **The rate held.** At 7.40%, unchanged, the first week without an increase after seven straight rises.
- **Price held.** The median sold price rose 0.7% to $439,000 and homes sold for 100.0% of final list price.
- **Sellers kept cutting.** Price reductions rose 2.6% to 1,884, or 4.60 for every contract that went pending.
- **Contracts slipped.** Pending contracts fell 5.3% to 410, still inside the 408 to 445 range of the last four weeks.

If you are buying, a reduced listing plus a seller credit toward closing costs or a rate buydown can do more for your payment than waiting on the rate. If you are selling, the data says buyers are shopping for the cuts, so price for this week's buyers.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Classic Baked Mac and Cheese

The first cold snap calls for comfort food. This is the old-fashioned version: a real cheese sauce, a crunchy buttery top, and nothing you need to hunt for. About 45 minutes, most of it in the oven.

<img src="/images/uploads/classic-baked-mac-and-cheese.jpg" alt="Golden baked macaroni and cheese in a white casserole dish with a spoon lifting a cheesy serving, on a rustic wood table with a teal linen napkin" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 8**

- 1 pound elbow macaroni
- 4 tablespoons butter, plus 2 tablespoons melted for the topping
- 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
- 3 cups whole milk, warmed
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon dry mustard
- Pinch of cayenne (optional)
- 4 cups sharp cheddar, grated, divided
- 1 cup Monterey Jack, grated
- 1 cup panko breadcrumbs

1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees and butter a 9 by 13 inch baking dish. Boil the macaroni 2 minutes less than the package says, then drain.
2. Melt the 4 tablespoons butter in a large pot over medium heat. Whisk in the flour and cook 1 minute. Slowly whisk in the warm milk and cook, stirring, about 5 minutes, until the sauce thickens.
3. Take the pot off the heat. Stir in the salt, pepper, mustard and cayenne, then 3 cups of the cheddar and all of the Monterey Jack until smooth.
4. Fold in the macaroni and pour it into the baking dish. Top with the remaining 1 cup cheddar.
5. Toss the panko with the 2 tablespoons melted butter and scatter it over the top.
6. Bake 25 to 30 minutes, until bubbling and golden. Rest 5 minutes before serving.
