A screenshot landed on Elena's phone at 9:40 on Sunday night. It was a spreadsheet with one cell circled in red. "Rate is 7.24," Omar had typed under it. "Does this still kill the duplex?"

He and Tess wanted a first rental, a Dundalk duplex listed at $329,000. They had run the numbers at 7.32 last week and the cash flow came out thin. The rate had fallen again, but only by eight basis points, and Tess was leaning toward waiting for a rate with a 6 in front of it.

Elena called at 8 the next morning. "That is two drops in a row, but the rate is not the number I would watch," she said. "Maryland price cuts fell to 1,691 from 1,891. In Baltimore County it was 192 cuts against 140 closings, with 93 contracts. Sellers are starting to hold. The bargains are real, and they are getting harder to find."

Tess asked it straight. "So if we wait, what does the duplex cost us?" Elena did not guess at the rate. "Probably the discount. Sellers who are done cutting will trade a few thousand dollars for certainty." She suggested $323,000, six thousand under the ask, with a seven-day inspection window and settlement in 21 days.

"What if they just say no?" Omar asked. "Then we know by Wednesday, your pre-approval is still good, and there is a second duplex in Essex to see Friday."

The sellers countered Tuesday night at $325,000 and kept the 21-day close. Omar and Tess accepted before breakfast on Wednesday, and Omar sent Elena a photo of the signed page: "We were going to wait for a number that was never coming. Twenty-one days was the cheaper trade."

> When price cuts fall 10.6% and the rate drops for a second week, the buyer who offers clean terms gets the deal before the discount disappears.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of October 26 to November 1, 2026, the Maryland mortgage rate fell for the second week in a row and price reductions dropped. The median sold price was $442,053, up 2.8% from $429,900 on a like-for-like re-pull. Closed sales reached an early count of 1,082, up 11.4% from 971 on a like-for-like count, helped by month-end settlements, and new listings were 1,842, which is 1.70 new listings for every sale. Pending contracts rose 1.3% to 452, while price reductions fell 10.6% to 1,691, or 3.74 for every contract that went pending. The median time from listing to signed contract for homes that closed was 26 days, against 22 on last week's re-count. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate fell to 7.24% from 7.32%. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 48 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Edging Up), up one point. Anne Arundel County had the tightest supply of the large counties at 1.31 new listings per closing, while Baltimore City carried the most at 2.74. For buyers, fewer cuts means discounts are shrinking, so offer on reduced homes now. For sellers, price for this week's buyers.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### The Rate Fell Again

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 7.24%, down from 7.32%, the second weekly decline in a row. On a $400,000 loan that is about $22 a month less than last week, and the payment is still about $155 a month higher than at the 6.66% low this report tracked in late August.

### Closings Jumped on a Month-end Week

1,082 Maryland homes closed this week, against 971 at the same point last week on a like-for-like re-pull, up 11.4%. This week runs through the end of October, when many contracts written in September reach settlement, so treat the jump as a month-end bump, not a new trend. Total closed volume was about $565.4 million.

### Price Rose

The median sold price was $442,053, up 2.8% from last week's $429,900 on a like-for-like re-pull. Homes that closed sold for a median 98.0% of their original list price and 100.0% of final list price.

### Contracts Held Steady

Pending contracts rose 1.3% to 452, from 446. Pending is the forward-looking number, and it has now held above 440 for two weeks in a row.

### Sellers Cut Less

Price reductions were 1,691, against 1,891 last week, down 10.6%. That is 3.74 price cuts for every contract that went pending, down from 4.24 last week because cuts fell while contracts held.

### Speed Slowed a Little

Median days to contract was 26, against 22 on last week's re-count. The median home that closed this week took 22 days on market, up from 19, and new listings came in at 1,842, down 9.2% from 2,029, or 1.70 new listings for every sale.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of October 26 to November 1, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market edging up.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 48 out of 100, up one point from last week's reading of 47.

- **The rate fell again.** At 7.24%, down from 7.32%, the second weekly decline in a row.
- **Price rose.** The median sold price rose 2.8% to $442,053 and homes sold for 100.0% of final list price.
- **Contracts held.** Pending contracts rose 1.3% to 452, above 440 for the second week.
- **Sellers cut less.** Price reductions fell 10.6% to 1,691, or 3.74 for every contract that went pending, down from 4.24.

If you are buying, discounts on reduced homes are shrinking, so get your pre-approval ready and move when the right one appears. If you are selling, the data says confidence is returning, so price for this week's buyers and list into the first weekend.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Classic Chicken and Dumplings

The first week of November is the time for a pot of something creamy and warm. This is the classic: tender chicken, carrots and celery in a rich broth, topped with fluffy drop dumplings. About an hour, and it feeds a crowd.

<img src="/images/uploads/classic-chicken-and-dumplings.jpg" alt="A bowl of chicken and dumplings with carrots, celery and parsley on a rustic wood table with a teal linen napkin" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 6**

- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1 large yellow onion, diced
- 3 carrots, sliced
- 3 stalks celery, sliced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/3 cup all-purpose flour
- 6 cups chicken broth
- 1 1/2 pounds boneless skinless chicken thighs
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1/2 cup frozen peas
- Dumplings: 2 cups all-purpose flour, 1 tablespoon baking powder, 1 teaspoon salt, 3 tablespoons cold butter, 1 cup milk
- Chopped fresh parsley for serving

1. Melt the butter in a large Dutch oven over medium heat. Cook the onion, carrots and celery 8 minutes, until softened. Add the garlic and cook 1 minute.
2. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute. Slowly whisk in the broth, then add the chicken, thyme, salt and pepper.
3. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer, covered, 20 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through. Shred the chicken in the pot with two forks and stir in the peas.
4. For the dumplings, whisk the flour, baking powder and salt. Cut in the cold butter with your fingers until crumbly, then stir in the milk until just combined.
5. Drop spoonfuls of dough on top of the simmering stew. Cover and cook 15 minutes without lifting the lid, until the dumplings are puffed and cooked through.
6. Sprinkle with parsley and serve hot.
