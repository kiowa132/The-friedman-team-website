Carla Reyes had her Carroll County house ready to list back in July. Her agent talked her out of it.

"Everyone's telling you the same thing right now," he said. "Wait for after Labor Day. Buyers come back to school-year mode, sellers who were sitting on the fence all list at once, and you don't want to be one more house nobody's looking at in the middle of August."

So she waited. And on September 8th, her listing went live, right along with 2,365 other Maryland sellers who had the exact same idea. Statewide new listings jumped 17.4% in a single week, the biggest one-week supply increase this report has tracked. Carla watched the portal that first weekend and felt her stomach drop a little. Hundreds of new listings, all competing for the same buyers, in the same seven days.

Here's what she didn't see: that same week, homes that actually made it to the closing table sold for a median 99.6% of their original asking price, up from 99.0% the week before. Fewer homes closed overall, mostly a holiday-week settlement lull, attorneys and title companies don't love scheduling closings around Labor Day, but the ones that did close held their price better than they had in weeks.

Her agent priced her house at what the Carroll County comps actually supported, not what she hoped a bidding war might produce. Within four days, against a flood of new competition, she had a signed contract at 98% of ask.

> The market absorbed a historic wave of new supply in a single week without sellers losing pricing power. If you've been waiting for "after Labor Day" like everyone else, you are not late. You're on time, along with a lot of company.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of September 7 to 13, 2026, Maryland's housing market posted its largest one-week new-listing surge on record while closed sales fell sharply, a combination driven mostly by the Labor Day holiday sitting inside the reporting week. New listings jumped 17.4% to 2,366, at a median asking price of $448,383 (up 6.8%), while closed sales fell 38.4% to 708, the lowest weekly total this report has tracked, largely reflecting fewer settlements scheduled around the September 7th holiday. Pending contracts, a cleaner forward-looking demand read, slipped a much milder 7.1% to 481. Critically, sellers who did close gained ground: the median sold price rose 1.1% to $445,000, and closed homes realized a median 99.6% of their original list price, up from 99.0% the week before. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 6.76%, its third straight weekly increase. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 48 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Pricing Power Returns). St. Mary's County was the fastest-moving county in the state at a 14-day average, while Prince George's and Montgomery counties saw new listings build fastest relative to what's actually closing. For buyers, this is a week with meaningfully more choice. For sellers, it means pricing to the comps still works, even against the heaviest competition of the year.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### Closings Fell Hard, and the Calendar Is the Reason

Closed sales across Maryland totaled 708 for the week, down 38.4% from 1,150 the prior week, the lowest weekly total this report has tracked. September 7th was Labor Day, and title companies and attorneys' offices routinely avoid scheduling settlements on or immediately around the holiday, which compresses closings into the surrounding weeks rather than through the holiday week itself. Pending contracts, the better forward-looking read on demand, fell a much milder 7.1% to 481, consistent with a holiday-week pause rather than a genuine break in demand. Total closed sales volume for the week, adjusting for a Howard County data-entry error described below, was approximately $375,960,939.

### Maryland Saw Its Largest New-Listing Surge on Record

New listings hit 2,366 for the week, up 17.4% from 2,016, the biggest one-week jump this report has measured. The median price on those new listings came in at $448,383, up 6.8% from $419,900 the week before. This is the "wait for after Labor Day" seller instinct playing out statewide, all at once, sellers who held back over the summer coming to market together, and pricing with confidence rather than testing low. It's also part of why price reductions actually fell 4.1% to 2,011: a wave of freshly priced new listings dilutes the pool of stale, previously-discounted ones.

### Sellers Who Closed Gained Pricing Power

Despite the wave of new competition, homes that closed this week did better on price, not worse. The median sold price rose 1.1% to $445,000, and the median ratio of sold price to original list price improved to 99.6%, up from 99.0% the week before. Median days to contract also ticked down slightly, from 21 to 20. Put together: a historic new-listing surge did not cost sellers leverage on the homes that actually transacted.

### Mortgage Rates Rose for a Third Straight Week

The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate averaged 6.76% for the week, up five basis points from 6.71%, the third consecutive weekly increase. For a buyer financing a $445,000 purchase with 20% down, moving from 6.71% to 6.76% adds roughly $12 to the monthly principal-and-interest payment. The trend, three straight weeks higher, matters more than any single week's move, and it is the main headwind working against the extra inventory buyers gained this week.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of September 7 to 13, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market, with buyers gaining choice and sellers holding price.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 48 out of 100, down two points from last week, but the internal picture is more interesting than the headline number.

- **Buyers gained real choice.** 2,366 new listings is the largest weekly total this report has tracked, a genuine expansion in what's available to shop, concentrated especially in Prince George's, Montgomery, and Baltimore City, where new supply is building fastest relative to what's closing.
- **Sellers held, and even gained, pricing power.** The median sold-to-original-list ratio rose to 99.6%. Sellers are not discounting to compete with the new wave; if anything, the data says the market absorbed it without a price concession.
- **The wildcard remains the mortgage rate.** At 6.76% and rising for a third straight week, affordability is tightening at the margin, which will eventually cap how much of this new inventory converts to closed sales.

If you are buying, this is a week with meaningfully more to see, but financing costs are moving against you, so get pre-approved and shop with urgency once you find the right house. If you are selling, the data says you can price to your comps with confidence even into a crowded market.

## County Spotlights

### Carroll County Real Estate Market

Carroll County closed 21 homes this week at a $449,000 median, a step back from the top of the pace leaderboard after being at or near the top for much of August. The average days on market reads 27, with a median of 15, still a fast-moving county, just no longer the fastest. New listings in Carroll jumped to 55 this week, giving buyers considerably more to choose from than they've had in weeks. For a county that is Kyle Friedman's home base, this reads less like cooling demand and more like a market catching its breath alongside the rest of the state during the post-Labor-Day supply wave.

### Talbot County Real Estate Market

Talbot County posted the highest median sold price of any Maryland county this week, $875,000 on 13 closings, with a 52-day average days on market that reflects the smaller, choosier buyer pool that high-end Eastern Shore inventory always draws. Talbot is also home to this week's priciest active listing, a $9,950,000 waterfront estate on the Tred Avon River. New listings in the county came in at 31, at a median asking price approaching $750,000, evidence that the luxury Eastern Shore market is still testing higher prices even as volume stays thin.

### Allegany County Real Estate Market

Allegany County posted the lowest median sold price in the state this week, $117,950 on 6 closings, and is also home to this week's most affordable active listing, a $15,000 manufactured home on a rented lot in Frostburg. New listings ticked up to 26 at a median of $182,450. Allegany remains Maryland's clearest entry-level market, useful context for the statewide $445,000 median, which sits on top of an enormous range of inventory.

### St. Mary's County Real Estate Market

St. Mary's County was the fastest-moving market in Maryland this week, a 14-day average days on market across 13 closings, at a $470,000 median. That is genuine speed on a meaningful sample. With 37 new listings this week against just 4 pending contracts, there's a real gap between new supply and confirmed demand worth watching, but for now, homes that do sell in St. Mary's are moving quickly.

### Prince George's County Real Estate Market

Prince George's County posted the most lopsided new-listings-to-closings ratio in the state this week, 281 new listings against just 49 closings, a 5.7x ratio well above the 3.3x statewide average. Pending contracts, at 57, were among the highest in the state, so demand is present, but the sheer volume of new supply landing at once gives Prince George's buyers real room to negotiate over the next several weeks as that inventory works through the pipeline.

### Montgomery County Real Estate Market

Montgomery County recorded the highest closing volume in the state this week at 93 homes, and the highest county median among large-volume counties at $625,000, with a 25-day average days on market, among the fastest large counties in Maryland. Montgomery also led the state in raw new-listing volume, 420 for the week, a 4.5x ratio against closings. Montgomery remains Maryland's largest housing market, and this week's numbers show both strong absorption and a substantial new wave of choice arriving for buyers at once.

### Anne Arundel County Real Estate Market

Anne Arundel County closed 86 homes this week at a $500,000 median, a 26-day average days on market that puts it among the five fastest-moving counties in the state. New listings came in at 215, a 2.5x ratio against closings, right around the statewide pace, so supply and demand are staying roughly in balance here even as other counties see a bigger imbalance. Annapolis, Severna Park, Glen Burnie, Pasadena, and Crofton all continue to see steady activity, and waterfront and water-access communities around the Chesapeake Bay and Severn River remain some of the county's most competitive inventory.

### Baltimore County Real Estate Market

Baltimore County closed 83 homes this week at a $372,500 median and a 29-day average days on market, one of the higher-volume counties in the state. New listings jumped to 248 for the week, a 3.0x ratio against closings, in line with the statewide average. Baltimore County spans a wide range of price points, from starter rowhomes and townhomes in the southwestern and eastern communities to larger single-family inventory in Towson, Lutherville-Timonium, Cockeysville, and the Greenspring Valley, and this week's numbers reflect that broad base rather than any one submarket driving the county.

### Frederick County Real Estate Market

Frederick County closed 49 homes this week at a $455,000 median and a 29-day average days on market, keeping pace with the statewide speed. New listings reached 134, a 2.7x ratio against closings, close to the statewide average of 3.3x, suggesting Frederick's new supply is being absorbed a bit more efficiently than the state as a whole. Frederick continues to draw buyers priced out of Montgomery County who want a shorter commute than the far exurbs, with the City of Frederick, Urbana, New Market, and Middletown anchoring most of the week's activity.

### Harford County Real Estate Market

Harford County closed 44 homes this week at a $389,500 median, though its 48-day average days on market is a step slower than the pace it posted a few weeks ago. New listings came in at 100 for the week, a 2.3x ratio against closings, the tightest ratio among Maryland's larger counties, meaning new supply here is being absorbed closer to the pace it's arriving than almost anywhere else in the state. Bel Air, Abingdon, Fallston, Havre de Grace, and Aberdeen remain the county's steadiest submarkets for buyers looking for more square footage within commuting range of Baltimore.

### Howard County Real Estate Market

Howard County closed 44 homes this week at a $595,000 median, one of the highest county medians among large-volume markets, with a 28-day average days on market. New listings hit 107 for the week, a 2.4x ratio against closings, on the tighter end statewide. Columbia, Ellicott City, and Clarksville continue to anchor a county that consistently ranks among Maryland's priciest and most competitive, and this week's pace confirms demand there hasn't slowed with the season.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: End-of-Garden Pico de Gallo

The last real tomatoes of the season are still coming off Eastern Shore vines right now, before the first cool snap ends the run. This is the week to use them: a simple, fresh pico de gallo that lets a countertop full of end-of-summer tomatoes speak for itself.

<img src="/images/uploads/maryland-real-estate-market-report-week-of-september-7-13-2026-pico-de-gallo.png" alt="Before and after: diced tomatoes, onion, and jalapeno in a bowl, then mixed into finished end-of-garden pico de gallo" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Makes about 3 cups**

- 4 to 5 ripe tomatoes, diced, seeds and excess liquid removed
- 1/2 cup white onion, finely diced
- 2 jalapenos, minced (seeds removed for less heat)
- 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, chopped
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- Juice of 2 limes
- Salt, to taste

1. Combine the tomatoes, onion, jalapenos, cilantro, and garlic in a bowl.
2. Squeeze in the lime juice and season with salt to taste.
3. Let it sit at room temperature for 15 to 20 minutes so the flavors come together before serving.

Scoop with tortilla chips or spoon over grilled chicken, fish, or eggs. Best eaten the same day, while the tomatoes are still garden fresh.
