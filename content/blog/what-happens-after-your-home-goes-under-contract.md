But despite what many homeowners believe, \*\*your home isn't officially sold yet.\*\*



In real estate, there's an old saying: \*The first offer gets you excited. The closing table gets you paid.\*



Between accepting an offer and handing over the keys, there are several important steps that must happen. Home inspections, appraisals, financing, title work, and final walkthroughs can all affect whether your transaction closes on time or at all.



The good news is that understanding what comes next can help reduce stress and prevent costly surprises.



Whether you're selling your first home or your fifth, this guide walks you through exactly what happens after your home goes under contract in Maryland.



\---



\# What Does "Under Contract" Actually Mean?



When your home goes under contract, it means you've accepted an offer and both buyer and seller have signed a legally binding purchase agreement.



At this stage, the buyer has committed to purchasing your home but most contracts include contingencies that still need to be satisfied before the sale becomes final.



Think of it as reaching halftime in a football game. You're ahead, but the game isn't over.



Most Maryland home sales close within \*\*30 to 45 days\*\*, although cash purchases can close much faster.



\---



\# Step 1: The Earnest Money Deposit



Shortly after the contract is signed, the buyer submits an earnest money deposit.



This isn't an extra fee it's a good-faith deposit showing they're serious about purchasing your home.



The funds are typically held in escrow by the title company or brokerage until settlement.



If everything goes according to plan, the earnest money is applied toward the buyer's purchase at closing.



If the buyer backs out for a reason protected under the contract, they may receive their deposit back. If they default without a contractual reason, they could lose it.



\---



\# Step 2: The Home Inspection



The home inspection is often the first major milestone after going under contract and it's also where many sellers become nervous.



A licensed home inspector evaluates the property's major systems, including:



\* Roof

\* Foundation

\* Plumbing

\* Electrical

\* HVAC

\* Windows

\* Appliances

\* Attic

\* Crawl space

\* Exterior



Depending on the property, buyers may also order additional inspections such as:



\* Radon testing

\* Well water testing

\* Septic inspection

\* Termite inspection

\* Mold inspection



It's important to remember that \*\*no home is perfect.\*\*



Even newer homes usually generate inspection reports with dozens of items. Most are minor maintenance issues rather than major defects.



\---



\# Step 3: Repair Negotiations



Once inspections are complete, the buyer may request repairs or financial credits.



This doesn't necessarily mean you'll have to fix everything.



Common outcomes include:



\* Completing agreed-upon repairs

\* Offering a seller credit toward closing costs

\* Reducing the purchase price

\* Splitting repair costs

\* Selling the property completely as-is



Every transaction is different.



The goal isn't always to "win" negotiations it's to keep the transaction moving toward a successful closing while protecting your financial interests.



\---



\# Step 4: The Home Appraisal



If the buyer is financing the purchase with a mortgage, their lender will usually require an appraisal.



Many homeowners confuse the appraisal with the inspection.



They're actually very different.



The inspection looks at the home's condition.



The appraisal estimates the home's market value.



The appraiser compares your property to recently sold homes with similar characteristics.



Three outcomes are possible:



\### The Home Appraises Above the Purchase Price



Great news.



The transaction usually continues without issue.



\### The Home Appraises Exactly at Value



This is the most common scenario.



The sale proceeds normally.



\### The Home Appraises Below the Contract Price



This is where additional negotiations may occur.



Possible solutions include:



\* Seller lowering the price

\* Buyer bringing additional cash

\* Both parties negotiating a compromise

\* Requesting a reconsideration of the appraisal



A low appraisal doesn't automatically mean the sale falls apart.



Many transactions successfully move forward after some negotiation.



\---



\# Step 5: Loan Underwriting



While inspections and appraisals are taking place, the buyer's lender is working behind the scenes.



The underwriting department reviews:



\* Income

\* Employment

\* Assets

\* Credit history

\* Debt

\* Bank statements

\* Tax returns

\* Appraisal

\* Insurance



Sometimes additional documentation is requested before final approval.



This is completely normal and happens in many transactions.



\---



\# Step 6: The Title Company Gets to Work



The title company plays one of the most important roles in the transaction.



Their job is to make sure the property's ownership can legally transfer to the buyer.



They'll verify:



\* Ownership history

\* Existing liens

\* Property taxes

\* Mortgage payoff

\* Judgments

\* Easements

\* HOA information (if applicable)



If any issues arise, they're usually addressed before settlement.



\---



\# Step 7: Preparing for Closing



As settlement approaches, both buyer and seller begin preparing for moving day.



As the seller, this is a good time to:



\* Schedule movers

\* Notify utility companies

\* Gather garage door openers

\* Find appliance manuals

\* Collect house keys

\* Organize warranties

\* Forward your mail

\* Finish agreed-upon repairs



It's also important to avoid making unnecessary changes to the property after going under contract.



The buyer expects to receive the home in substantially the same condition they agreed to purchase.



\---



\# Step 8: The Final Walkthrough



Typically held within 24 to 48 hours before closing, the buyer conducts a final walkthrough.



This isn't another inspection.



Instead, it's the buyer's opportunity to confirm:



\* Repairs were completed

\* The home is in expected condition

\* Included appliances remain

\* No new damage has occurred



If everything looks good, the transaction moves toward settlement.



\---



\# Step 9: Closing Day



Closing day is when ownership officially changes hands.



Depending on your settlement company, you may sign documents in person or electronically.



During closing:



\* Final paperwork is signed.

\* Loan funds are transferred.

\* Existing mortgages are paid off.

\* Closing costs are deducted.

\* Ownership is recorded.

\* Sale proceeds are distributed.

\* The buyer receives the keys.



Congratulations your home is officially sold.



\---



\# Typical Timeline After Going Under Contract



While every sale is different, here's what many Maryland sellers can expect:



\*\*Day 1:\*\* Offer accepted and contract signed



\*\*Days 1–3:\*\* Earnest money deposited



\*\*Days 5–10:\*\* Home inspection completed



\*\*Days 10–15:\*\* Repair negotiations



\*\*Days 15–25:\*\* Appraisal ordered and completed



\*\*Days 20–35:\*\* Loan underwriting and final approval



\*\*Days 30–45:\*\* Closing and settlement



Cash transactions may close much faster, while more complex financing can take longer.



\---



\# Can a Buyer Back Out?



Yes but only under certain circumstances.



Most Maryland contracts include contingencies that protect the buyer.



Common contingencies include:



\* Home inspection

\* Financing

\* Appraisal

\* Title issues

\* Home sale contingency



If these contingencies aren't satisfied, the buyer may have the right to terminate the contract according to its terms.



\---



\# Can the Seller Back Out?



Generally, it's much more difficult for a seller to walk away once a contract has been signed.



Doing so could expose the seller to legal consequences or financial damages.



If circumstances change unexpectedly, it's important to speak with your real estate agent before making any decisions.



\---



\# What Can Delay Closing?



Even well-managed transactions can experience delays.



Some of the most common reasons include:



\* Financing issues

\* Low appraisal

\* Inspection disputes

\* Title problems

\* Missing paperwork

\* HOA delays

\* Employment verification

\* Utility issues

\* Weather-related complications

\* Scheduling conflicts



Most delays are temporary and can be resolved with good communication.



\---



\# Five Things Sellers Should Never Do After Going Under Contract



Many homeowners unintentionally create problems during this stage.



Avoid these common mistakes:



\*\*Don't cancel your homeowners insurance early.\*\*



Keep your policy active until ownership officially transfers.



\*\*Don't turn off utilities.\*\*



Inspectors, appraisers, and buyers may still need electricity and water.



\*\*Don't start major renovations.\*\*



Unexpected changes can create new issues.



\*\*Don't throw away paperwork.\*\*



Keep receipts for any repairs you've agreed to complete.



\*\*Don't stop maintaining the property.\*\*



Continue mowing the lawn, cleaning, and caring for the home until settlement.



\---



\# Frequently Asked Questions



\## Is "under contract" the same as sold?



No.



A home is under contract after an offer is accepted, but it isn't officially sold until closing is complete.



\## How long does it take to close after accepting an offer?



Most financed transactions close within 30 to 45 days, although cash sales may close in as little as one to two weeks.



\## What happens if the appraisal comes in low?



The buyer and seller can renegotiate the purchase price, the buyer can bring additional cash, or both parties may reach another agreement.



\## Do sellers have to make every repair requested?



No.



Repairs are negotiated between the buyer and seller. Some sellers agree to repairs, others provide credits, and some sell completely as-is.



\## When do I receive my money?



After settlement is completed and the transaction has funded, your proceeds are typically sent by wire transfer or check, depending on your settlement company.



\---



\# The Bottom Line



Going under contract is one of the biggest milestones in the home-selling process but it's not the finish line.



The weeks between accepting an offer and closing involve inspections, financing, title work, negotiations, and careful coordination between multiple parties. While unexpected challenges can arise, most transactions move smoothly when they're managed proactively.



If you're preparing to sell your home in Maryland or your home is already under contract and you have questions having an experienced real estate professional by your side can make the process far less stressful.



Every home sale is unique, and knowing what to expect at each step helps you make informed decisions with confidence.



Whether you're selling a starter home, a waterfront property, or a luxury estate, understanding what happens after going under contract is one of the best ways to ensure a successful closing.
