Maryland home sales pulled back sharply this week, but the story underneath the headline number is more encouraging than it looks, especially if you're a buyer who got outbid a few times earlier this summer. Here's the full county by county breakdown of what happened in the Maryland real estate market between August 3 and August 9, 2026, including current mortgage rates, median home prices, and which Maryland counties are moving fastest right now.



The Story of the Week: Why Slower Closings Are Good News for Buyers



Three weeks ago, a buyer of mine, I'll call her Priya, lost a house in Eldersburg in Carroll County. She and her husband had been house hunting since spring, got outbid twice, and the third time they stretched to offer $18,000 over asking, cash difference out of pocket since it wouldn't appraise that high. They still lost. Nine offers came in on that one house. She called me afterward, discouraged, and asked whether she should just wait until next year to buy a home in Maryland.



I told her not yet. I told her to give it a few weeks.



This week, I'd tell her to start looking again. Statewide, closed home sales dropped to 530 homes, down sharply from 782 the week before, the quietest week this report has tracked all summer. Price reductions ticked up to 1,158 active listings, and the median sold price fell to $405,000, down $35,000 from two weeks prior. For a buyer like Priya, that's not a warning sign. That's the Maryland housing market finally exhaling after a hot summer.



Here's the detail that actually matters: pending contracts barely moved, landing at 371 for the week, almost identical to last week's 369. That tells us buyers who are ready to transact are still closing deals, at a normal, unhurried pace, and average days on market held rock steady at 30 days. Nobody's panic bidding. The nine offer bidding war Priya lost in June is considerably less likely to happen to a Maryland home buyer this week.



Mortgage rates did climb again, to 6.69%, a sixth consecutive weekly increase according to Freddie Mac. That part isn't good news. But affordability and buyer competition are two separate fights, and this week, the competition side of that equation got noticeably easier for Maryland home buyers.

What This Week's Numbers Really Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers



Closings fell hard, but the buyer pipeline didn't. Statewide closed sales dropped 32.2% week over week, the steepest single week decline in recent Friedman Report history, while pending contracts held essentially flat. That divergence is the real signal: last week's closing surge was a backlog of existing contracts finishing up, not a new wave of demand, and this week shows what happens once that backlog clears out. A closings drop without a matching drop in pending contracts points to a temporary pipeline correction, not a genuine pullback in buyer activity.



Maryland mortgage rates keep climbing. The 30 year fixed mortgage rate hit 6.69% this week, according to Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey, a sixth consecutive weekly increase and the highest level of the current run. Six weeks ago, this same rate sat comfortably under 6.6%. That's real, cumulative pressure on monthly affordability for Maryland home buyers, even though it isn't showing up as panic in any single week's transaction data.

Deep Dive: The Surprising Fact About Maryland Home Prices This Week



Here's a statistic that runs counter to almost everything you'd expect from a week that just posted a 32% drop in closings: Maryland is one of the only states in the country where the median list price sits below the median sold price.



A recent six month statewide analysis found Maryland homes closed at a median of $415,000 against a median list price of $401,990, a gap of $13,010, roughly 3.1%, with sold prices running above what sellers originally asked. In most states, that spread runs the opposite direction, sellers ask more than buyers ultimately pay. Maryland runs the other way, which is the statistical signature of competitive, multiple offer bidding still happening underneath the market's day to day headlines. That same analysis found Maryland's pending to active listing ratio sitting around 0.62, meaning roughly 62 homes are already under contract for every 100 active listings on the market, among the strongest demand readings of any state measured nationally.



One important caveat: that figure comes from a different six month dataset and methodology than this week's weekly Bright MLS pull used elsewhere in this report, so it shouldn't be read as a direct week to week comparison. But it explains something real happening underneath this week's numbers: even as Maryland's closing volume dropped hard, this week's own sold to list ratio held at exactly 100%. Correctly priced Maryland homes are still selling for precisely what they're asking, even in a week where the headline numbers looked soft.
