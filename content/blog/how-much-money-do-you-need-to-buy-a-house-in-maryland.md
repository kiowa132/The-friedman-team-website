**Quick answer:** most Maryland buyers need between 5% and 10% of the purchase price in total cash - covering the earnest money deposit, inspections, appraisal, down payment, and closing costs - though the exact number depends on your loan program, purchase price, and county. On a $450,000 home, that typically lands between $25,000 and $45,000 all-in, not counting moving costs.

> It's not one lump sum due at closing. It's a series of payments, spread across the entire time you're under contract.

That number gets thrown around constantly, and almost nobody breaks down where it actually comes from. Some of it's refundable, some isn't, and it starts moving the moment your offer gets accepted, not the day you close.

Below is every dollar, in the order you'll actually spend it, specific to buying in Carroll, Baltimore, Howard, or Frederick County.

## 1. The Earnest Money Deposit (Your First Dollars Spent)

The earnest money deposit (EMD) is the first money that leaves your account, usually within one to three business days of your offer being accepted - well before you know if the home will pass inspection or appraise correctly.

**How much is earnest money in Maryland?**

In Carroll County and Central Maryland, the standard benchmark is 1% of the purchase price. In competitive, multiple-offer situations, buyers often increase that to 2-3% to strengthen their offer. On a $450,000 home, that's $4,500 to $13,500.

This isn't an extra fee - if you close, it's credited directly toward your down payment and closing costs. But it needs to be liquid and ready to move fast, and it's almost always sent by wire transfer to the title company or escrow account rather than a personal check. Wire transfers typically carry a $25-$50 fee from your bank, and if you wire EMD and then wire closing funds separately later, that fee hits twice.

**A safety note worth repeating to every buyer:** never trust wiring instructions received only by email. Wire fraud targeting real estate closings is common, and a wire can't be reversed once sent. Always confirm instructions by phone using a number you already know is legitimate.

## 2. Home Inspection Costs (And Why This Is the Riskiest Money You'll Spend)

**How much does a home inspection cost in Maryland?**

A standard Structural and Mechanical inspection - covering the foundation, roof, HVAC, plumbing, and electrical systems - typically runs $300 to $600 in Maryland, depending on the size and age of the home.

**What inspections can you get in Maryland?**

The Maryland REALTORS® Property Inspections Addendum lays out the full list of inspections a buyer can request:

- Structural and Mechanical (the standard inspection)
- Radon
- Wood-Destroying Insect (termite)
- Mold
- Environmental
- Chimney
- Lead-Based Paint
- Other (well, septic, sewer scope, or anything property-specific)

Most buyers order Structural and Mechanical only. But depending on the property, it's common to add radon (especially relevant in older homes with basements, which describes much of the housing stock across Carroll, Baltimore, and Frederick Counties), termite, or well and septic inspections for homes not on public water and sewer.

**Here's the part that catches buyers off guard:** every inspection is paid out of pocket, upfront, directly to the inspector - not rolled into your loan, not paid at closing. And if the deal falls through after you've paid for it, for any reason, that money does not come back. A buyer ordering Structural only might spend $400 total. A buyer adding radon, termite, and septic could spend $800-$1,000 - and if that specific contract falls apart, none of it carries over to the next house.

Budget for the real possibility of paying for inspections more than once. In a competitive market, losing a contract after inspection isn't rare - it's a normal part of the process.

## 3. The Appraisal Fee

**How much does a home appraisal cost in Maryland?**

A standard single-family home appraisal in Maryland typically costs $400 to $700, with larger or more complex properties running higher.

Like the inspection fee, this is non-refundable. Your lender orders the appraisal to confirm the home is worth what you're paying, usually collecting the fee at the time of application. If the appraisal comes in low and the deal falls apart, or either side walks away for another reason, that money is gone regardless of outcome.

## 4. The Down Payment

**How much do you need to put down on a house in Maryland?**

Down payment requirements vary significantly by loan program:

- **Conventional loans:** as low as 3% for qualified first-time buyers, more commonly 5-20%
- **FHA loans:** 3.5% minimum
- **VA loans:** 0% down for eligible veterans and active-duty service members
- **USDA loans:** 0% down in eligible rural areas, which includes parts of Frederick and Carroll County

Your exact number depends on your loan program, credit profile, and lender - that conversation belongs with a mortgage professional, not your real estate agent. But understanding the range helps explain why two buyers looking at the same house can need dramatically different amounts of cash to get there.

## 5. Closing Costs - And Why They're Different by County

**How much are closing costs in Maryland?**

Maryland buyers typically pay between 2% and 6% of the purchase price in closing costs, though most transactions land closer to 4% as a realistic middle. On a $450,000 home, that's roughly $9,000 to $27,000, with $18,000 as a reasonable expectation.

A major reason Maryland's closing costs run higher than many states: buyers pay both a state transfer tax and county-level transfer and recordation taxes, and the rates change meaningfully depending on which county you're buying in.

That's a real, comparison-shifting difference. Choosing between similarly priced homes in Baltimore County versus Carroll County can mean a full percentage point of difference in transfer and recordation taxes alone.

**First-time buyer exemption:** First-time Maryland homebuyers are exempt from the state's 0.5% transfer tax on the first $500,000 of purchase price, which can save up to $2,500.

Who pays which portion - buyer or seller - isn't fixed by law. It's negotiated in the contract and varies by local custom county to county. Don't assume; confirm what's typical for your specific county and get it in writing in your offer.

## 6. Prepaid Costs (Escrow at Closing)

This part doesn't feel like a "fee," but it's still real money leaving your account at closing. Lenders collect several months of property taxes and homeowners insurance upfront into an escrow account, so you're not hit with one large tax bill later.

- **Property taxes:** Maryland's average effective rate is about 1.09%, roughly $4,000/year on a $370,000 home
- **Homeowners insurance:** averages around $1,100/year statewide, though actual quotes vary by home and coverage
- **Private mortgage insurance (PMI):** if you're putting down less than 20% on a conventional loan, expect roughly 0.50%-1.00% of your loan amount annually - not a closing cost, but a real addition to your monthly payment

## 7. Moving Costs

The least standardized number in this whole breakdown. A local move with a rental truck runs a few hundred dollars; a full-service local move is more often in the low thousands; long-distance moves change the math entirely. Getting a couple of quotes once your move date is set is the fastest way to a real number - faster than any article can give you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much money do I need saved to buy a house in Maryland?**

Most buyers need between 5% and 10% of the purchase price in total cash to cover the earnest money deposit, inspections, appraisal, down payment, and closing costs combined. On a $450,000 home, that's typically $25,000 to $45,000, not including moving costs.

**Is earnest money the same as a down payment?**

No. Earnest money is a good-faith deposit paid within days of your offer being accepted, typically 1% of the purchase price in Carroll County and Central Maryland. It's credited toward your down payment and closing costs at closing, but it's paid separately and much earlier in the process.

**Do I get my inspection or appraisal money back if the deal falls through?**

No. Both inspection fees and appraisal fees are paid for services already rendered and are non-refundable, regardless of why the deal doesn't close.

**Which Maryland county has the lowest closing costs?**

Among Carroll, Baltimore, Howard, and Frederick Counties, Carroll County has the lowest combined transfer and recordation tax burden at roughly 1.5%, compared to roughly 2.5% in Baltimore County.

**Are Maryland closing costs paid by the buyer or the seller?**

It depends on the contract and local custom, and varies by county. Transfer and recordation taxes, in particular, are negotiable between buyer and seller - confirm what's typical in your specific county with your agent before writing an offer.
