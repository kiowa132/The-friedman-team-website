Picture a couple named Greg and Amy. They love their Carroll County home, and they have found a bigger one two miles away that they love more. Now they are stuck on the question everyone asks in this spot: do we sell ours first, or buy the new one first?

There is no answer that works for everybody. There is an answer that works for you, and it depends on what you value most: certainty, or convenience.

**Quick answer:** Sell first if you want certainty. You will know your exact budget before you fall in love with a house, and you will never carry two mortgages. Buy first if you are financially secure, want a single move, and can qualify for both loans. When you cannot get either to work cleanly, a contingency, a rent-back, or a short-term loan can bridge the gap.

## What Does It Look Like to Sell First and Then Buy?

The sequence is simple:

1. We run a market analysis and set a price.
2. You prepare the home and list it.
3. You accept an offer and close the sale.
4. You shop for your next home with the money in hand.

## How Do You Get at Your Equity Before You Sell?

If you want to buy first but your money is tied up in the old house, there are four common tools. They are separate products, not one loan that does everything.

- **HELOC (home equity line of credit).** Usually the lowest-cost option, but many lenders will not open one on a home that is already listed for sale, so the decision has to be made weeks before your first open house.
- **Home equity loan.** A lump sum against your equity, with a fixed rate.
- **Bridge loan.** Short-term financing that can also help with qualifying for the new mortgage. It is the most expensive of the four, with rates commonly in the high single digits to low teens and an origination fee on top.
- **Cash-out refinance.** Replaces your existing mortgage with a larger one. With many homeowners holding a low rate from earlier years, that is often the wrong trade.

Which one, if any, makes sense depends on your rate, your equity, and your timeline. Talk to your lender before you commit to a plan. For the full menu of ways to bridge the gap, read [the two-mortgage problem](/blog/the-two-mortgage-problem-maryland-move-up-buyers).

## What About Making an Offer Contingent on Selling Your Home?

A home sale contingency lets you buy the new home only if your current one sells. It lowers your personal risk, but it can make your offer less competitive because the seller is being asked to wait on a sale that has not happened yet.

In Maryland, sellers typically protect themselves with a kick-out clause. The seller keeps marketing the home, and if a better offer comes in, you get 72 hours to remove your contingency or the contract ends. In a busy market with multiple buyers, that is a real handicap. In a slower market or on a home that has been sitting, it can work well. Our guide to [the clause that wins bidding wars](/blog/the-clause-that-wins-bidding-wars-escalation-and-appraisal-gaps-maryland) covers how contingencies play in competitive situations.

## Can a Rent-Back Help You Avoid Two Moves?

Often, yes, and it is one of the best fixes for the biggest complaint about selling first. In a post-settlement occupancy agreement, also called a rent-back, you stay in your home for a set period after closing and pay the new owner a daily fee. They typically last up to about 60 days, because much longer can conflict with the buyer's loan requirement that they occupy the home. The buyer usually holds a security deposit through the title company.

A rent-back gives you time to find your next home without a hotel stay, but it has to be negotiated as part of the sale, so raise it before you list.

## What Mistakes Do Sellers Make When Sell-and-Buy Is on the Table?

These five come up again and again:

1. **Trusting an automated online value.** A Zestimate is not a market analysis. We looked at why in [what Zillow does not tell you](/blog/what-zillow-doesnt-tell-you-the-truth-about-zestimate-accuracy-in-maryland).
2. **Waiting too long to get advice.** The sooner we talk, the more options you have.
3. **Overestimating your equity.** Selling costs come out of it. Our guide on [whether you can afford to move up](/blog/can-you-afford-to-move-up-maryland-how-to-calculate-your-equity-and-budget) shows how to calculate the number that is actually yours.
4. **Spending on upgrades you will not get back.** Fix what buyers notice and price what they will pay for.
5. **Trying to line up two closings on the same day.** Perfect synchronization is not required, and chasing it adds stress. A rent-back or a short gap is usually easier.

## The Bottom Line

The right sequence is the one that matches how much uncertainty you can live with. Neither path is the wrong one. The mistake is picking without running the numbers, because your budget, your equity, and your lender all shape what is possible. Start by finding out what your home would net you, and the rest of the plan gets easier to see. Our [net proceeds calculator](/calculators/net-proceeds) is a good first step.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I sell my house before buying a new one?**

If you want certainty and a firm budget, yes. It is the simplest path and avoids paying two mortgages. If you are financially secure and want a single move, buying first can work, but you must be approved to carry both.

**Can I buy a new house before selling my current one?**

Yes, if your lender approves you for both payments, or if you use a HELOC, bridge loan, or another way to access equity. Some buyers make their offer contingent on selling their current home instead.

**What is a home sale contingency?**

A clause that makes your purchase depend on selling your current home. Sellers often add a kick-out clause, which gives you 72 hours to remove the contingency if they receive another offer they prefer.

**What is a rent-back agreement?**

An arrangement where the seller stays in the home for a short time after closing and pays the buyer a daily fee. It is usually limited to around 60 days.

**How do I avoid moving twice?**

Negotiate a rent-back with your buyer, plan a short gap between closings, or buy first if you qualify. Talk to your agent before listing, since most of these options have to be built into the contract.
