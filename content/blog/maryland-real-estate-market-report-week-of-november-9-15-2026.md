Priya had the plan written on the fridge calendar in red marker: "Start looking. January." Everyone told her and Marcus the same thing. Nobody lists a house the week before Thanksgiving, so why tour empty weeks?

On Monday their agent, Dana, sent one screenshot and no opinion. Baltimore County had 230 new listings this week, up from 184 the week before. Pending contracts had slipped to 77 from 88. "More houses, fewer people writing offers," she typed. "That is the opposite of what January usually looks like."

Wednesday night they scrolled through the new ones together. Thursday morning Freddie Mac put the 30-year rate at 7.11%, the fourth drop in a row. Marcus did the math on a napkin and stopped arguing about January.

Saturday they toured three homes in Catonsville. The third was a split-level that had come on the market Monday at $389,000, with a fenced yard and a basement Marcus had already furnished in his head. Nobody else was in the driveway.

Dana did not push them high. "With this many new listings, the seller is watching the calendar too," she said. They offered $380,000 and asked for $6,000 toward closing costs to buy the rate down.

Sunday afternoon the seller came back at $384,000 with a $5,000 credit, and Priya and Marcus signed before dinner. Priya took a photo of the fridge calendar, crossed out January, and sent it to Dana with one word: "Early."

> When new listings rise 7.1% and contracts fall 9.2% in the same week, the buyer who shows up before the holidays gets the choice and the credit.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of November 9 to 15, 2026, Maryland new listings rose and pending contracts fell, while the mortgage rate dropped for a fourth week in a row. The median sold price was $425,700, up 0.4% from $424,000 on a like-for-like re-pull. New listings were 1,671, up 7.1% from 1,560, and pending contracts fell 9.2% to 456. Price reductions fell 7.3% to 1,620, or 3.55 for every contract that went pending. Closed sales reached an early count of 762, against 859 for last week on a full re-count, and will rise as late entries post. The median time from listing to signed contract for homes that closed was 27 days, unchanged. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate fell to 7.11% from 7.18%. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 46 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Buyers Get Options), down two points. Howard County had the tightest supply of the large counties at 1.28 new listings per closing, while Baltimore City carried the most at 4.32 on an early closings count. For buyers, more homes and fewer competing offers means room to negotiate before the holidays. For sellers, price for this week's buyers.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### The Rate Fell for a Fourth Week

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 7.11%, down from 7.18%, the fourth weekly decline in a row. On a $400,000 loan that is about $19 a month less than last week, and the payment is still about $120 a month higher than at the 6.66% low this report tracked in late August.

### Sellers Listed Instead of Waiting

1,671 new listings came on the market across Maryland, up 7.1% from 1,560 last week. The median new-listing price was $399,000, nearly unchanged. The week before Thanksgiving week is usually when sellers start pulling back. This year more of them showed up.

### Buyers Stepped Back

Pending contracts fell 9.2% to 456, from 502. Pending is the forward-looking number, and it moved the opposite way from new listings, so buyers this week had more to choose from and fewer people competing for each home.

### Price Held

The median sold price was $425,700, up 0.4% from last week's $424,000 on a like-for-like re-pull. Homes that closed sold for a median 97.6% of their original list price and 100.0% of final list price.

### Sellers Cut a Little Less

Price reductions were 1,620, against 1,748 last week, down 7.3%. Because contracts fell faster, that is 3.55 price cuts for every contract that went pending, against 3.48 last week.

### Closings Are an Early Count

762 closings have posted so far this week, against 859 for last week on a full re-count, down 11.3%. This week's number was pulled on Sunday and closed sales keep posting for days after a week ends, so treat the drop as a timing gap, not a slowdown. Median days to contract held at 27.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of November 9 to 15, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market where buyers get options.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 46 out of 100, down two points from the last published reading of 48.

- **The rate fell again.** At 7.11%, down from 7.18%, the fourth weekly decline in a row.
- **Sellers listed.** New listings rose 7.1% to 1,671 the week before Thanksgiving week.
- **Buyers stepped back.** Pending contracts fell 9.2% to 456.
- **Price held.** The median sold price was $425,700, up 0.4%, and homes sold for 100.0% of final list price.

If you are buying, this is a week with more homes and fewer competing buyers, so tour now and negotiate for a credit. If you are selling, you have more competition than you expected, so price for this week's buyers and make the home easy to see over the holiday.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Sweet Potato Casserole with Pecan Crumble

Thanksgiving is less than two weeks away, so here is the side dish that disappears first. Creamy mashed sweet potatoes with a little vanilla and cinnamon under a crunchy brown sugar pecan topping. Make it a day ahead and bake it right before dinner.

<img src="/images/uploads/sweet-potato-casserole-with-pecan-crumble.jpg" alt="A baking dish of sweet potato casserole with a golden brown sugar pecan topping on a rustic wood table with a teal linen napkin" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 8**

- 3 pounds sweet potatoes (about 4 large)
- 1/2 cup granulated sugar
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 1/2 cup whole milk
- 4 tablespoons butter, melted
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- Topping: 3/4 cup packed brown sugar, 1/3 cup all-purpose flour, 4 tablespoons cold butter, 1 cup chopped pecans

1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees. Peel the sweet potatoes, cut them into chunks, and boil 15 to 20 minutes until fork tender. Drain well.
2. Mash the sweet potatoes until smooth. Stir in the sugar, eggs, milk, melted butter, vanilla, cinnamon and salt.
3. Spread the mixture in a buttered 9 by 13 inch baking dish.
4. For the topping, mix the brown sugar and flour, then work in the cold butter with your fingers until crumbly. Stir in the pecans.
5. Scatter the topping evenly over the sweet potatoes.
6. Bake 30 to 35 minutes, until the topping is golden and the edges bubble. Let it rest 10 minutes before serving.
