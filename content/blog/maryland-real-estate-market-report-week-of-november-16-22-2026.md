Gwen counted yard signs on her Tuesday morning walk through their Ellicott City neighborhood: two that said Sold, and not one new For Sale. She put the question to Ray before he finished his coffee. "Everybody lists in the spring. Why would we go now, with Thanksgiving next week?"

Their agent, Jess, came by that afternoon with one page from her weekly numbers. "Howard County had 55 new listings this week and 56 closings," she said. "More homes are selling than coming on. In April you are one of a crowd. Right now you are one of a few." She pointed to the statewide line: sellers were getting a median 98.1% of their original asking price.

Ray had his own doubt. "The rate just went back up to 7.28. Doesn't that scare buyers off?" Jess did not wave it away. "It thins the crowd a little, so we make the payment easier instead of the price lower." The plan: list the four-bedroom colonial at $599,000, keep Thanksgiving weekend open for showings, and be ready to offer a credit toward a rate buydown instead of a cut. "If nothing comes by the Monday after the holiday, we talk price then. You lose one week, not a season."

The house went live Thursday. Saturday brought five showings, and a couple moving up from a townhouse in Columbia came back Sunday for a second look.

Sunday night their agent sent an offer at $590,000, asking for $6,000 toward closing costs to buy down the rate. Jess countered at $596,000 with the full $6,000 credit. The buyers got their lower payment, and Gwen and Ray netted the same $590,000 the buyers had first offered. The buyers accepted Monday morning.

Gwen walked the same loop that afternoon and stopped at her own sign. She texted Jess a photo of it with one line: "Glad we didn't wait for the tulips."

> When the rate goes up and sellers still get 98.1% of their asking price, the seller who lists before spring is not early. They are on time.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of November 16 to 22, 2026, Maryland home prices rose even as the mortgage rate turned back up. The median sold price was $427,250, up 2.0% from $418,750 on a like-for-like re-pull, and homes sold for a median 98.1% of original list price. New listings fell 5.3% to 1,582 from 1,671, and pending contracts were 448, down 1.8% from 456. Price reductions were 1,600, down 1.2%, or 3.57 for every contract that went pending. Closed sales reached 918 on a Monday count, against 902 for last week on a full re-count, and will still rise as late entries post. The median time from listing to signed contract for homes that closed was 26 days. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 7.28% from 7.11%, the first increase after four declines. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 47 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Sellers Gaining Ground), up one point. Howard County was the only county with at least 10 sales where closings outnumbered new listings, at 0.98x, while Baltimore City carried the most supply of the large counties at 2.60x. For sellers, listing before spring is paying off. For buyers, shop the payment and ask for a credit.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### Prices Rose

The median sold price was $427,250, up 2.0% from last week's $418,750 on a like-for-like re-pull. Homes that closed sold for a median 98.1% of their original list price, up from 97.5%, and 100.0% of final list price.

### The Rate Turned Back Up

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 7.28%, up from 7.11%, the first increase after four weekly declines. On a $400,000 loan that is about $46 a month more than last week, and about $166 a month more than at the 6.66% low this report tracked in late August.

### Fewer Sellers Listed

1,582 new listings came on the market across Maryland, down 5.3% from 1,671 last week. The median new-listing price was $399,900. With Thanksgiving on Thursday, fewer sellers are adding homes, and the ones already listed face less new competition.

### Contracts Held

Pending contracts were 448, against 456 last week, down 1.8%. In a week the rate went up, buyers barely slowed.

### Price Cuts Were Flat

Price reductions were 1,600, against 1,620 last week, down 1.2%. That is 3.57 price cuts for every contract that went pending, about the same as 3.55 last week.

### Closings Were Counted a Day Later

918 closings had posted when this week's numbers were pulled Monday morning, a day later than usual, against 902 for last week on a full re-count, up 1.8%. Closed sales keep posting for days after a week ends, so this week's number will still rise. Median days to contract was 26, against 27 last week.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of November 16 to 22, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market where sellers are gaining ground.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 47 out of 100, up one point from the last published reading of 46.

- **Prices rose.** The median sold price was $427,250, up 2.0%, and homes sold for 98.1% of original list.
- **Fewer sellers listed.** New listings fell 5.3% to 1,582 the week before Thanksgiving.
- **Buyers stayed.** Pending contracts were 448, down only 1.8%.
- **The rate turned up.** At 7.28%, up from 7.11%, the first increase after four declines.

If you are selling, the week before the holiday rewarded the sellers who did not wait for spring, so price it right and keep it easy to show. If you are buying, the rate went up, so negotiate for a credit toward a rate buydown instead of only a lower price.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Green Bean Casserole from Scratch

Thanksgiving is Thursday, so here is the classic side made the real way: fresh green beans, a quick mushroom cream sauce instead of the can, and crispy onions on top. Build it Wednesday night and bake it while the turkey rests.

<img src="/images/uploads/green-bean-casserole-from-scratch.jpg" alt="A baking dish of green bean casserole with a golden crispy onion topping on a rustic wood table with a teal linen napkin" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 8**

- 2 pounds fresh green beans, trimmed and cut in half
- 4 tablespoons butter
- 8 ounces cremini mushrooms, sliced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 cup chicken broth
- 1 cup half and half
- 1 teaspoon salt and 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 cups crispy fried onions, divided

1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees. Boil the green beans in salted water for 5 minutes, then drain and rinse with cold water.
2. Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Cook the mushrooms 6 to 8 minutes until browned, then add the garlic for 1 minute.
3. Sprinkle in the flour and stir for 1 minute. Slowly whisk in the broth and half and half and simmer 3 to 4 minutes until thick. Season with salt and pepper.
4. Stir in the green beans and 1 cup of the fried onions, then spread everything in a buttered 9 by 13 inch baking dish.
5. Bake 20 minutes, top with the remaining fried onions, and bake 5 minutes more until golden and bubbling.
