"We'll wait until after the holidays to list." It is one of the most common things Maryland homeowners say in November, and it makes sense. The house is full of family, the calendar is packed, and nobody wants to stage a living room while the turkey is in the oven. But the buyers still touring between Thanksgiving and New Year's are worth a closer look.

**Quick answer:** selling over the holidays in Maryland means fewer buyers but more serious ones. In the week of November 9 to 15, 2026, new listings rose 7.1% to 1,671 while pending contracts fell 9.2% to 456. The buyers still out have a deadline: a lease ending, a new job, a pre-approval. If your home is priced for this week's buyer, easy to show, and lightly decorated, the holidays can mean less competition than spring, when every seller who waited lists at once.

## Why holiday buyers are serious buyers

People who shop for a home in late November and December usually have a reason they cannot put off: a lease that ends in January, a job that starts after New Year's, a growing family, or a pre-approval with an expiration date. They tour fewer homes and they decide faster. The question for a seller is not whether buyers exist in December. It is whether your home is ready for the ones who do.

## How to sell your house over the holidays

**Price for this week's buyer, not spring's.** The most expensive holiday mistake is listing at a spring price in December to see what happens. With more homes coming on and fewer contracts, an overpriced home sits, and a home that sits through the holidays looks stale in January. If you are deciding between a price cut and a buyer incentive later, see [Seller Credit or Price Cut?](/blog/seller-credit-or-price-cut-maryland).

**Decorate light.** A wreath, a few candles and a small tree photograph warm. A tree blocking the main window, lawn inflatables and decor on every surface hide the house. Keep walkways, windows and counters clear.

**Make it easy to show.** Holiday buyers often tour on short notice: a day off, a long weekend, a relative watching the kids. Block only the times that truly do not work, use a lockbox, and say yes to everything else.

**Turn on the lights and the heat.** It is dark by 5 p.m. Open the blinds during the day, turn on every light before an evening showing and keep the house warm. A cold, dim house feels smaller than it is.

**Plan for slower holiday timelines.** If you go under contract in December, inspections, appraisals and lender work may move a few days slower around the holidays. Build a little room into the settlement date.

## Should you wait until spring instead?

Sometimes. If the home needs real repairs, if you cannot keep it show-ready with family in town, or if your next home is not lined up, waiting can be the right call. Listing once and listing right beats rushing. For when prices actually peak and dip through the year, read [The Full Year, Broken Down](/blog/the-full-year-broken-down-when-home-prices-actually-peak-and-dip).

But if you are ready, the holidays are not a dead zone. You trade a smaller crowd for more motivated buyers and less competition from the sellers who decided to wait.

## The Bottom Line

Fewer buyers shop between Thanksgiving and New Year's, but the ones who do are motivated. If you plan to sell in the next few months, the right price and an easy-to-show home matter more than the date on the calendar.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it a bad idea to sell a house during the holidays?**
Not if the home is priced right and easy to show. There are fewer buyers, but they tend to be motivated, and there is less competition from other sellers than in spring.

**Do houses sell in December in Maryland?**
Yes. Buyers with leases ending, job moves or expiring pre-approvals keep shopping through December, and homes priced for the current market still go under contract.

**Should I decorate my house for the holidays when it is for sale?**
Lightly. A few tasteful pieces make listing photos and showings feel warm, but heavy decor hides rooms, windows and floor space.

**Is it better to list before or after the holidays?**
If your home is ready, listing before or during the holidays means less competition. If it needs work or your next move is not lined up, waiting for January or spring can make more sense.

**How do I handle showings with family staying over?**
Block the times that truly do not work, such as holiday meals, and keep the rest of the week open. A lockbox and clear showing instructions help you avoid missing a serious buyer.
