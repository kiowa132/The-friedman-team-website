I am 28 years old, and I sell houses. Lately I have noticed something at my showings. The buyers walking through the homes I show in Parkville, Nottingham, and Perry Hall are more and more people my age, the ones who were in school when the iPhone came out.

They are not what the headlines said they would be. They are showing up with pre-approvals, real questions, and a plan.

**Quick answer:** Gen Z accounted for a record 20 percent of home purchase rate locks in the second quarter of 2026, according to ICE Mortgage Technology, and nearly a third of first-time buyer loans. They are buying earlier than older generations did, they are looking at more affordable areas like Carroll County and outer Baltimore County, and they want a starter home with light, a clean kitchen, and a yard, not a dream house.

Those figures come from ICE Mortgage Technology's July 2026 Mortgage Monitor, which tracks rate locks on purchase mortgages. A rate lock is not a closing, but it is a strong signal of who is actually in the market. Roughly one in five people locking a rate to buy a home is from Gen Z.

## Why Is Gen Z Buying Earlier Than Millennials Did?

They watched what happened to the generation ahead of them. A lot of Millennials waited for interest rates to fall back to 3 percent, and while they waited, prices kept climbing. Gen Z looked at that and came to a conclusion: waiting is not always the safe choice. Sometimes it is the expensive one.

We looked at the same question from the other side in [five myths about buying your first home](/blog/5-myths-about-buying-your-first-home-maryland-first-time-buyer-guide). The cost of waiting is real, even when it does not feel like a cost.

## Where Are Younger Buyers Looking?

Remote and hybrid work changed the map. If you do not have to sit in a downtown office five days a week, you can look past the most expensive close-in neighborhoods and find more house for the money. That is why I am seeing young buyers in places their parents would not have considered at the same stage of life, like Carroll County and outer Baltimore County.

The trade is familiar: a longer drive on the days you go in, in exchange for a price you can actually carry. ## How Are Gen Z Buyers Paying for It?

Creatively, and with help. ICE's data shows the average Gen Z borrower locking a purchase loan in the second quarter had a credit score of 722, the lowest of any generation but well within reach of most loan programs. Industry reports also show Gen Z relying on government down payment assistance more than other generations. And nationally, gifts, loans, and retirement withdrawals now make up about 29 percent of purchase down payments, the highest share in seven years, though most 2026 buyers still rely mainly on personal savings.

For Maryland buyers, that often means combining a low-down-payment loan with the state's down payment assistance. Ask a lender early what you qualify for.

## What Should Gen Z Buyers Do First?

1. **Get pre-approved.** It tells you a real budget and makes your offer credible.
2. **Look at more than one area.** A twenty-minute change in commute can change what you can afford.
3. **Ask about assistance programs.** Maryland has several, and they can be combined with the right loan.
4. **Run the full monthly number,** including taxes and insurance, not just the mortgage.
5. **Do not wait for a perfect market.** Buy the house you can afford now, and plan to stay long enough to make it worthwhile.

## The Bottom Line

If you are in your twenties and thinking you are too early, or that everyone else has it figured out, you are not early and you are not alone. One in five buyers locking a mortgage right now is your age. The question is not whether people your age can buy a home. It is which home fits you and how to structure the purchase so it works.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are Gen Z buyers really buying homes?**

Yes. According to ICE Mortgage Technology, Gen Z made up a record 20 percent of home purchase rate locks in the second quarter of 2026 and nearly a third of first-time buyer loans.

**Why is Gen Z buying earlier than Millennials?**

Many saw older generations wait for lower rates while prices kept rising. Remote work also lets younger buyers look at more affordable areas farther from job centers.

**What do Gen Z buyers look for in a home?**

Usually a starter home with good light, a clean kitchen, and a nice yard, plus a strong online presentation with quality photos and video.

**Where are young buyers looking in Maryland?**

Many are looking in Carroll County and outer Baltimore County, in places like Parkville, Nottingham, and Perry Hall, where their money goes further than closer in.

**How can a young buyer afford a first home in Maryland?**

By combining a low-down-payment loan, such as FHA or conventional with 3 percent down, with Maryland's down payment assistance where eligible, and by shopping across more than one area. A lender can show you exactly what you qualify for.
