The real story is a full wave that builds, peaks, drops, and starts climbing again and knowing where you are in that wave changes how you should think about timing.



Here’s the month by month breakdown, based on ATTOM’s analysis of over 52 million home sales from 2015 through 2025, tracking the “seller premium” how much above estimated market value homes actually sold for, month by month.









Q1 (January to March): The Climb

This is where momentum builds, not where it starts flat. January already sits at a solid 9.6% better than most of the back half of the year. February pushes to 10.0%, and March is the single strongest month of the entire year at 10.7%.



What this means: if you’re weighing whether to list in late winter versus waiting for “real spring,” the data says don’t wait. Buyer demand and pricing power are already climbing in February, and March is your peak month, not April or May like a lot of sellers assume.



Q2 (April to June): The Plateau

This is the window everyone already knows about, and it holds up April and May both sit at 10.2%, and June is close behind at 10.0%. It’s less a single spike and more a strong, sustained plateau across three months.



What this means: the “spring market” isn’t one moment you can miss by a few weeks. You have roughly a 90 day window where pricing power stays consistently high, which gives you real flexibility on exactly when within that stretch you list.









Q3 (July to September): The Slide

This is where the year turns. July drops to 8.5%, August to 8.4%, and September down to 8.0%. It’s a steady, predictable decline not a cliff, but a clear and consistent giveback of the pricing power sellers had all spring.



What this means: if you’re on the fence in early summer, earlier is better than later. A June closing still catches the tail end of peak season; a September closing means you’re already giving up more than two full points of premium compared to March.



Q4 (October to December): The Bottom, Then the Turn

October is the low point of the entire year at 7.9%. But the wave doesn’t just stay flat at the bottom it starts climbing again almost immediately. November ticks up to 8.3%, and December jumps further to 9.1%, already ahead of where July and August sat.



What this means: this is the part most sellers miss. The market isn’t “dead” from October through the new year it’s turning the corner. A well priced home listed in December is already benefiting from rising momentum, even though most of your competition assumes nobody’s buying over the holidays.



Why the Wave Moves This Way

A few forces are stacked on top of each other every year:



School calendars pull buyer urgency toward a spring purchase and a summer move, which is why demand and pricing power builds through Q1 and holds through Q2.



Fatigue and distraction set in over summer and into fall as the serious early year buyers close out and the pool thins, which drives the Q3 slide.



Holiday buyers are unusually motivated. Sellers who list in November and December tend to be more serious (job relocations, life changes, year end closings), and the buyers still shopping in that window are less casual too which is part of why premiums start recovering before spring even arrives.



What This Actually Means for Your Timeline

If you have full flexibility: aim to list in February or early March. You’re catching the climb before the crowd of spring sellers floods the market, and you’re ahead of the peak competition months of April and May.



If you’re already mid year: don’t wait for “next spring” if you can help it. Every month from July on gives back a little more ground. Listing in July still beats listing in September.



If you’re facing a fall or winter sale: don’t assume you’re stuck with October’s number. November and December are already trending back up a home priced right and presented well in December is working with rising momentum, not against it, even if it doesn’t feel that way looking out at gray skies.

The One Thing the Data Doesn’t Capture

None of these numbers replace a well priced, well presented home. A strong listing in October will still outperform a mispriced one in March. But if you have any control over your timeline at all, this full year wave is worth building your plan around instead of just picking “sometime in spring” and hoping for the best.
