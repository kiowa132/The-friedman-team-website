Maya had eleven days before her new job started in Annapolis, and a flight landing Thursday night.

Her agent had spent the week narrowing the list to two homes in two counties. One was in Prince George's County, listed three weeks and still sitting. The other was in Anne Arundel County, a four-bedroom listed at $525,000 on Wednesday, close to the county's median new-list price of $542,500.

"Same state, same mortgage rate, completely different clocks," her agent told her Friday morning, over coffee, before the first showing. In Anne Arundel, 198 homes had hit the market that week while 164 had closed, about 1.2 new listings for every sale. In Prince George's County it was 1.8. The Prince George's house would still be there Monday. The Anne Arundel house would not.

Maya loved the Anne Arundel house in the first ten minutes. The kitchen was done, the backyard backed to trees, and the commute was twelve minutes. She felt the pull to sleep on it. Her agent did not tell her what to do. He laid out the math instead: a 7.28% rate was not going to change her payment by waiting a weekend, but a second showing on Saturday morning could change who else was in the room.

She wrote the offer from the driveway that night. Full price, a seven-day inspection window, and a closing date that flexed around her start date. Nothing to negotiate, nothing for the seller to say no to.

By Saturday morning, two more showings were already on the calendar. The seller accepted Maya's offer at nine, before either of them walked through. She locked her rate Monday and started her job on schedule.

She did not find the cheapest house in Maryland. She found the right house in the county where right houses move fast, and she was the one who was ready.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of September 28 to October 4, 2026, Maryland sellers stopped cutting prices and the median sold price recovered. The median sold price rose 3.4% to $439,450, recovering more than half of last week's drop, while price reductions fell 20.9% to 1,843 from last week's record 2,329. The median time from listing to signed contract for homes that closed fell to 22 days from 26. Closed sales reached 1,304 in a week that included quarter-end, and new listings rose 7.7% to 2,192, but pending contracts fell 8.3% to 408, the second weekly decline in a row. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 7.28%, up 25 basis points, its sixth straight weekly increase and the highest reading this report has tracked. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 45 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Recovering), up eleven points from last week's low. Carroll County was the fastest-moving large county at a 25-day average, while Washington County and Baltimore City carried the most new supply relative to closings. For buyers, which county you are in decides how fast you need to move. For sellers, the number you pick on day one matters more than any rate headline.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### Sellers Stopped Cutting

1,843 Maryland sellers reduced their asking price this week, down 20.9% from last week's record 2,329. After the biggest repricing wave this report has tracked, the pace cooled sharply.

### Price Came Back Up

The median sold price rose 3.4% to $439,450, recovering more than half of last week's $24,900 drop. Homes that closed sold for a median 98.7% of their original list price and 100.0% of their final list price, so sellers are getting very close to their number again.

### Time Sped Up

The median time to contract fell to 22 days from 26, the fastest reading since the week of September 7 to 13. The median home that closed this week took 20 days on market.

### Closings Jumped, Partly on the Calendar

Closed sales reached 1,304 and total closed volume was about $683.5 million, up from $443.0 million. Part of that is the quarter-end bunching of closings, so treat the size of the jump as an upper bound on the real change in demand.

### But Contracts Slipped

Pending contracts fell 8.3% to 408, the second weekly decline in a row. Pending is the forward-looking number, and it is the one to watch over the next two weeks.

### The Rate Keeps Climbing

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 7.28%, up from 7.03%, the sixth consecutive weekly increase. On a $400,000 loan, the move from last week alone adds roughly $68 a month, and measured against the 6.66% low this report tracked in late August it is closer to $166 a month more.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of September 28 to October 4, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market that is recovering.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 45 out of 100, up eleven points from last week's reading of 34, the lowest this report has logged.

- **Sellers regained ground.** The median sold price rose 3.4% and price reductions fell 20.9%.
- **Time sped up.** Days to contract went from 26 to 22.
- **Contracts are the soft spot.** Pending contracts fell 8.3% for the second straight week, so buyers are not rushing back yet.
- **The rate keeps climbing.** At 7.28% and rising six weeks straight, affordability pressure is real and building.

If you are buying, know your county before you decide how fast to move: in the tight counties a clean offer on day one wins, and in the loose ones you have room to negotiate. If you are selling, the data says the sellers who priced right on day one did not have to cut at all.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Maple Dijon Sheet Pan Chicken Thighs

The first cool evenings of October are sheet pan season. This one is a single
pan, about an hour start to finish, and the maple and Dijon glaze does the
heavy lifting.

<img src="/images/uploads/maple-dijon-sheet-pan-chicken-thighs.jpg" alt="Sheet pan of maple Dijon glazed chicken thighs with roasted sweet potato cubes and Brussels sprouts, fresh thyme, and a teal linen napkin on a rustic wood table" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 4 to 6**

- 3 pounds bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 8)
- 1 1/2 pounds sweet potatoes, peeled and cut into 1-inch cubes
- 1 pound Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
- 3 tablespoons olive oil, divided
- 1 1/2 teaspoons salt, divided, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1/4 cup maple syrup
- 3 tablespoons Dijon mustard
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme

1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees. Toss the sweet potatoes with 1 1/2
   tablespoons of the olive oil, 3/4 teaspoon of the salt, and the pepper on a
   large rimmed sheet pan. Roast 10 minutes.
2. While the potatoes roast, whisk the maple syrup, Dijon, garlic, and
   paprika in a bowl. Pat the chicken dry and season with the remaining
   3/4 teaspoon salt. Toss the chicken in half of the glaze. Toss the Brussels
   sprouts with the remaining 1 1/2 tablespoons olive oil and a pinch of salt.
3. Push the potatoes to one side of the pan. Add the chicken skin side up and
   the Brussels sprouts, tuck in the thyme, and return the pan to the oven.
4. Roast 30 to 35 minutes, brushing the remaining glaze over the chicken for
   the last 5 minutes, until the skin is deep golden and a thermometer in the
   thickest part of a thigh reads at least 175 degrees.
5. Rest 5 minutes, then serve straight from the pan.
