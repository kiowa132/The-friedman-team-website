Andre had been watching the same three-bedroom colonial in Baltimore County for six weeks, ever since his lender pre-approved him back in early August. Every time he called his agent for an update, the answer was the same: still active, still $459,900, still more than he wanted to carry at a rate that kept climbing every time he checked it.

On Wednesday, that changed. The seller cut the price to $429,900, one of 2,329 Maryland sellers who reduced an asking price this week, the most this report has tracked in a single week. Andre's agent called him within the hour.

He toured it Thursday evening. By then, Freddie Mac had already announced the 30-year rate at 7.03%, the fifth straight weekly increase and the first time this report has tracked it above 7%. Seven percent felt like a number Andre did not want attached to his name for the next three decades, and he almost talked himself out of making an offer at all.

His agent walked him through what the rest of the market was doing instead. The home he was standing in had already given up $30,000 to reach a price buyers would actually pay, and the median sold price across Maryland had fallen 5.5% this week alone, the sharpest drop this report has tracked. Waiting for the rate to come back down meant competing against a price that might not stay this low.

Andre wrote an offer that night at $421,000, four thousand under the new list price. The seller countered once, to $425,000, and Andre accepted Friday afternoon. He locked his rate that same day.

He did not get the rate he wanted. He got the price he wanted, on a home that had already done six weeks of price discovery before he ever wrote an offer. In a week when a record number of sellers cut their price, the buyers who moved fastest on the ones that already had were the ones who came out ahead.

> Sellers repriced at a record pace, and buyers who moved on the cuts came out ahead. This is a market resetting its price expectations before it resets anything else.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of September 21 to 27, 2026, Maryland's median sold price fell 5.5% to $425,000, the sharpest weekly drop this report has tracked, while a record 2,329 sellers cut their asking price, up 30.4% from the week before. Closed sales fell 5.2% to 933, new listings fell 10.3% to 2,035, and pending contracts fell 7.7% to 445, the first week all three dropped together. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 7.03%, up 8 basis points, its fifth straight weekly increase and the first reading above 7% this report has tracked. Homes that actually closed still sold for a median 98.5% of their original list price, essentially flat from 98.4%, so the price concession is showing up in active listings getting repriced, not in tougher negotiating at the closing table. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 34 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Cooling Sharply), the lowest reading this report has logged. Anne Arundel County was the fastest-moving large county at a 21-day average, while Baltimore City and Charles County carried the most new supply relative to closings. For buyers, this is a week to go after homes that already got reduced. For sellers, the data says do not wait to see if this passes.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### Price Gave Up More Ground Than Time Did

The median sold price fell 5.5% to $425,000, the largest single-week drop this report has tracked. Median days to contract barely moved, up just one day to 26. This was not a market where homes suddenly sat unsold, it was a market where sellers accepted less to get them sold on schedule.

### A Record Wave of Price Cuts

2,329 Maryland sellers reduced their asking price this week, up 30.4% from 1,786 the week before, the sharpest one-week jump this report has tracked. Sellers who launched too high in August and early September are now meeting the market where it actually is.

### The Concession Is Happening Before Closing, Not at the Table

Homes that actually closed this week sold for a median 98.5% of their original list price, essentially flat versus 98.4% last week, and 100.0% of their final list price, unchanged. The price giveback shows up in listings getting repriced while still active, not in buyers beating down accepted offers. By the time a deal reaches the closing table, sellers are still getting their (adjusted) asking price.

### Activity Cooled Across the Board

Closed sales fell 5.2% to 933, new listings fell 10.3% to 2,035, and pending contracts fell 7.7% to 445. Total closed volume was about $443.0 million, down from $533.0 million.

### The Rate Crossed a Line Buyers Notice

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 7.03%, up from 6.95%, the fifth consecutive weekly increase and the first week this report has tracked a rate above 7%. On a $400,000 loan, the move from last week alone adds roughly $22 a month; measured against the 6.66% low this report tracked in late August, it is closer to $100 a month more.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of September 21 to 27, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market that is cooling sharply.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 34 out of 100, down ten points from last week and the lowest reading this report has logged.

- **Price is giving way.** The median sold price fell 5.5% and price reductions jumped 30.4%, both the sharpest moves this report has tracked in either direction.
- **Time is barely moving.** Days to contract went from 25 to 26, a mild shift compared with everything else this week.
- **The rate crossed 7%.** At 7.03% and rising for a fifth straight week, affordability pressure is real and building.

If you are buying, sellers who already repriced this week have done your negotiating for you, go after those homes first. If you are selling, the data says do not wait: homes are not sitting dramatically longer, but the ones still priced for August are the ones getting cut.

## Maryland's 11 Biggest Housing Markets This Week, Plus Carroll County

Maryland's 11 largest counties by closings made up 86% of the state's sales this week. Here is how each one moved, plus Carroll County, Kyle Friedman's home market, every week regardless of rank.

### Montgomery County Real Estate Market

The state's biggest market closed 126 homes at a $600,000 median, down 6.6% from $642,215. Homes took slightly longer, a 35-day average versus 37 (essentially flat), and new listings fell 22.8% to 291, easing the new-listing-to-closing ratio to 2.31x from 2.5x.

### Prince George's County Real Estate Market

116 closings at a $451,438 median, down 4.3%. The average days on market slipped to 35 from 33, and new listings rose 3.5% to 292, a 2.52x ratio, down from 2.7x as closings picked up.

### Baltimore County Real Estate Market

115 closings at a $365,750 median, down 4.0%, with the average days on market jumping to 37 from 27, the biggest pace swing among the largest counties. New listings fell 7.6% to 254, a 2.21x ratio.

### Anne Arundel County Real Estate Market

91 closings at a $525,000 median, down 1.4%, and the fastest large market in the state this week at a 21-day average, down from 34. New listings fell 13.3% to 176, a 1.93x ratio.

### Baltimore City Real Estate Market

90 closings at a $245,500 median, up 8.9%, with a 45-day average, flat. It carries the most supply relative to closings among the large counties, 3.04x, with 274 new listings.

### Frederick County Real Estate Market

67 closings at a $470,000 median, down 10.9%. New listings rose 12.4% to 109, easing Frederick's supply ratio to 1.63x from 1.4x, and the average days on market slowed to 47 from 37.

### Howard County Real Estate Market

55 closings at a $585,000 median, down 16.4% from $700,000, the sharpest county-level price move among the largest markets. A 26-day average held steady, and new listings fell 5.4% to 87, a 1.58x ratio.

### Harford County Real Estate Market

49 closings at a $391,000 median, down 8.0%, with a 31-day average, close to last week's 30. New listings fell 18.1% to 77, a 1.57x ratio, among the tightest of the large counties.

### Worcester County Real Estate Market

34 closings at a $352,000 median, down 24.1% from $464,200, and the slowest large market in the state at an 87-day average, up from 66. New listings fell 9.7% to 65, a 1.91x ratio. The resort market is cooling hard as the season ends.

### Washington County Real Estate Market

30 closings at a $342,450 median, down 4.9%, with a 29-day average, close to flat. New listings fell 16.2% to 57, a 1.9x ratio.

### Charles County Real Estate Market

28 closings at a $466,500 median, up 9.8%, though the average days on market stretching to 50 from 59 is actually an improvement. New listings fell 10.7% to 75, a 2.68x ratio, the highest among the largest counties after Baltimore City.

### Carroll County Real Estate Market

25 closings at a $530,000 median, up 47.2% from $360,000 last week, though medians in a small county swing hard with the mix of homes that close in a given week. Homes still sold at a median 99.6% of original list, and the average was a fast 25 days. New listings fell 20.9% to 53, a 2.12x ratio.

## Deep Dive: The 30.4% Jump in Price Reductions

**30.4%** is how much price reductions jumped this week, from 1,786 to 2,329, the sharpest one-week increase this report has tracked. It is also the real reason the median sold price fell 5.5% to $425,000.

Here is the mechanism. Homes that actually closed this week still sold for a median 98.5% of their original list price, essentially unchanged from 98.4% last week. Buyers are not beating down accepted offers any harder than before. What changed is which homes are closing: fewer of the ones still priced for August, more of the ones that already got a reality check while they were sitting active.

On a $450,000 home, a seller who repriced this week instead of next likely saved several weeks of carrying costs and a stack of buyers who quietly moved on. ## Bonus From My Kitchen: Old Bay Chicken Noodle Soup

With a rainy weekend rolling through Maryland, this is the one to have going on the stove. It leans on Old Bay instead of the usual poultry seasoning, and it comes together in about 40 minutes starting from a whole cut-up chicken.

<img src="/images/uploads/old-bay-chicken-noodle-soup.jpg" alt="Dutch oven of chicken noodle soup with carrots, celery, and Old Bay seasoning, a rain-streaked window in the background" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 6**

- 1 whole chicken (about 4 pounds), cut into pieces
- 10 cups water
- 1 tablespoon Old Bay seasoning, plus more to taste
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 3 carrots, sliced
- 3 celery stalks, sliced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 8 ounces wide egg noodles
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- Fresh parsley, chopped, for garnish

1. Place the chicken, water, Old Bay, and bay leaves in a large pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer and cook 25 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through.
2. Remove the chicken and set it aside to cool slightly. Strain the broth and return it to the pot, discarding the bay leaves.
3. While the chicken cools, heat the olive oil in a skillet over medium heat and cook the onion, carrots, and celery for 6 minutes, then add the garlic and cook 1 minute more. Add the vegetables to the broth.
4. Shred the chicken, discarding the skin and bones, and return the meat to the pot.
5. Bring the soup back to a simmer, add the egg noodles, and cook 8 to 10 minutes, until tender.
6. Season with additional Old Bay, salt, and pepper to taste.

Serve hot with crackers or a thick slice of bread while the rain keeps coming down outside.
