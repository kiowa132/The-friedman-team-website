The ranking is based on one thing

CNBC’s “2nd worst economy” ranking comes down to one category: job growth and GDP growth over one year. That’s it. And yes Maryland got hit hard on that specific number. The state lost roughly 49,900 jobs between March 2025 and March 2026, the steepest drop in the country, with somewhere around 29,000 to 31,000 of those being federal jobs. Unemployment climbed from 3.6% to 4.3%. That part is real, and it’s tied almost entirely to federal workforce cuts and a new state IT tax that pushed some employers out.



But one bad year in one category isn’t the same thing as “worst economy in America.” And the moment you look at any other ranking, the story falls apart.











Same state, same month, completely different rankings

CNBC has another study their actual “Top States for Business” ranking, which weighs ten categories instead of one. On that list, Maryland comes in 36th. Not second worst. 36th.



A separate WalletHub study puts Maryland at 20th overall for state economies. And buried in that same study: Maryland ranks 4th in the entire country for Innovation Potential. 4th.



So which is it? Second worst, 36th, or 20th with a top-5 innovation score? These aren’t three different states they’re three different ways of measuring the same one, and CNBC just happened to publish the version that made the scariest headline.



There’s more recent data too. Maryland added 3,300 jobs in January and February of this year alone, with private-sector job growth actually outpacing the national rate. The federal cuts hit fast and hit hard but the numbers coming out now look more like a one-time shock working its way through, not a new permanent direction.



What about people leaving?

There’s a real piece of this too Maryland lost about 18,500 residents to other states in the most recent year on record, offset only by international migration. But “Maryland” isn’t one place. Frederick County grew by double digits. Montgomery County crossed 1.1 million residents. Baltimore City and Prince George’s County saw declines. People aren’t fleeing the state they’re sorting within it, toward lower-cost, higher-growth counties, several of which are right in our backyard.

The Friedman Take

CNBC picked the one stat that made the worst headline. I’ll take the one that actually pays your mortgage.



Maryland’s economy had a rough year in one specific, narrow measurement driven by a federal cut that’s already showing signs of easing. Meanwhile, home prices in the Baltimore Metro are at record highs, sales are climbing, and single-family homes are moving in about a week. Those are both true at the same time, and only one of them determines what your house is worth.



If you’re a seller waiting for “the economy to get better” before you list you’re waiting on a number that doesn’t move your equity. If you’re a buyer hoping a scary headline means a slowdown you can take advantage of the data says otherwise.
