Picture a seller named Dana. She lists her Howard County home on a Monday, gets two showings that week, and by Saturday her listing is already five days old with nothing to show for it. The photos were great. The price was fair. The problem was the calendar, because the listing went live at the one point in the week when almost no buyer was planning anything.

For most sellers, "going live" feels like a single moment. For the sellers who get the strongest first weekends, it is the last step in a short, deliberate sequence.

**Quick answer:** Coming Soon is a Bright MLS status that lets us market your home to agents and buyers before showings begin, so interest is already building on launch day. It works when the days before launch are spent widening who knows about your home, not narrowing it. Done right, we enter Coming Soon about six days ahead, hold a Saturday open house as the payoff, and flip to Active on a Thursday so your listing lands right before buyers plan their weekend.

## What Does "Coming Soon" Mean on a Maryland Listing?

Coming Soon is not a private or "pocket" listing. Under Bright MLS's updated rules this summer, a listing can sit in one of four statuses, and the difference between them is exactly who can see the home and whether they can tour it.

Coming Soon sits in the middle for a reason. When we use it, the home is visible to the agents who represent buyers in your price range and neighborhood, but nobody can walk through it yet. That is the whole idea: visibility first, showings second.

## Does Coming Soon Help You Sell for More?

Honestly, it depends on how it is used, and the research is more careful than the sales pitch.

Bright MLS studied more than 100,000 home sales and found that homes that started as Office Exclusives, meaning hidden from the wider market, took about two weeks longer to go under contract than standard listings. It also found no evidence that they sold for more when comparing similar homes in similar neighborhoods.

That study is about keeping a home quiet, which is the opposite of what a well-run Coming Soon period does. The dividing line is simple. ## What Is the Best Day of the Week to List a Home in Maryland?

Thursday, or close to it. In Redfin's analysis, homes listed on a Thursday went under contract about five days faster than homes listed on a Sunday, and sold for about $3,015 more on average than homes listed on a Monday. On a $500,000 home, that gap works out to roughly $3,700.

The reason is timing and memory. Buyers do most of their touring on weekends, so a home that appears on Thursday is the freshest thing they saw right before they plan their Saturday. Wednesday and Friday work too. A Monday or Tuesday listing has to hold buyers' attention for days before the weekend arrives, and a Friday listing can be too late for someone to fit it into their plans.

## When Coming Soon Does Not Make Sense

Coming Soon is a tool for building interest, so it does not fit every listing.

- **Your home is priced above what the market supports.** More days of visibility will not fix a price problem. It will just delay finding out.
- **You want to keep the sale quiet.** If privacy is the goal, Bright's other statuses and the new options for keeping details off public portals are a different conversation, with different tradeoffs.
- **The home is not ready.** Coming Soon works when photos, condition, and pricing are already finished. Using it as a way to buy time while repairs drag on wastes the momentum.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Coming Soon status in Bright MLS?**

Coming Soon is a listing status that allows a home to be marketed before it is available to show. Showings and offers are not part of it, and the seller can choose how widely it is shared with other agents and public sites.

**Is Coming Soon the same as a private or pocket listing?**

No. A private or office-only listing keeps the home hidden from the wider market. A Coming Soon listing we run is shared through the MLS with agents, which is the opposite of hidden.

**How long should a home be in Coming Soon?**

We typically use about six days, which is long enough for agents and neighbors to react and short enough that momentum does not fade. The exact timing depends on your Saturday open house.

**What is the best day to list a home in Maryland?**

Thursday. Redfin's analysis found Thursday listings went under contract faster than Sunday listings and sold for more, on average, than Monday listings. Wednesday and Friday are solid fallbacks.

**Does Coming Soon get you a higher price?**

Not automatically. Bright MLS research found no price advantage for homes that started hidden as Office Exclusives, and they took longer to reach contract. Coming Soon helps when it widens who knows about your home before launch, not when it narrows it.
