Dale had the Freddie Mac page printed and flat on the kitchen table when Nan got home Sunday night. "7.32," he said. "It dropped Thursday. Do we list now, or do we wait for spring?"

They planned to sell the four-bedroom in Hampstead and move to a one-level townhouse near their daughter. Nan had always said spring, when the yard looks good. Their agent, Dana, came by Monday morning with her own printout.

"The rate fell eight basis points, the first drop since late August," Dana said. "And pending contracts across Maryland rose to 446, up from 410. Buyers are moving again. In Carroll County, 40 new homes came on the market last week while 43 closed. Fewer homes are arriving than selling, and spring is when everyone else lists."

Nan worried about the kitchen. "What if they see the old countertops and ask for $15,000 off?" Dana did not tell them to renovate. Carroll's median sold price was $471,000, so $485,000 was a fair reach for a four-bedroom. "List at $485,000, paint the front door, and offer a ten-day rent-back so you are not packing and closing in the same week. If nothing comes by Sunday night, we adjust Monday, and you have lost one weekend."

The house went live Thursday. Saturday brought six showings. By Sunday night there were two offers, one at full price that asked for the ten-day rent-back Dana had already offered. They signed Monday morning.

Nan stood in the empty kitchen with the signed page. "Full price, and ten more days in my own kitchen," she told Dana. "I would have waited until April for less."

> When the rate falls for the first time in two months and contracts jump 8.8%, the seller who lists into thin supply meets the returning buyers first.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of October 19 to 25, 2026, the Maryland mortgage rate fell for the first time since late August and pending contracts rebounded. The median sold price was $435,000, up 3.6% from $420,000 on a like-for-like re-pull. Closed sales reached an early count of 790, against 802 at the same point last week, and new listings were 2,029, which is 2.57 new listings for every sale. Pending contracts rose 8.8% to 446, while price reductions held at 1,891, or 4.24 for every contract that went pending. The median time from listing to signed contract for homes that closed was 22 days, against 24 on last week's re-count. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate fell to 7.32% from 7.40%. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 47 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Warming Slowly), up four points. Carroll County had the tightest supply of the large counties at 0.93 new listings per closing, while Baltimore City carried the most at 4.25. For buyers, more contracts means more competition for the reduced homes. For sellers, list into thin supply and price for this week's buyers.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### The Rate Slipped for the First Time

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 7.32%, down from 7.40%, the first weekly decline since the climb began in late August. On a $400,000 loan that is about $22 a month less than last week, and the payment is still about $177 a month higher than at the 6.66% low this report tracked in late August.

### Closings Are an Early Count

790 Maryland homes have closed so far this week, against 802 at the same point last week, down 1.5%. Last week's count has since climbed to 996 as late closings posted, so expect this one to rise too. Total closed volume so far is about $397.1 million.

### Price Edged Up

The median sold price was $435,000, up 3.6% from last week's $420,000 on a like-for-like re-pull. Homes that closed sold for a median 98.7% of their original list price and 100.0% of final list price.

### Contracts Rebounded

Pending contracts rose 8.8% to 446, from 410. Pending is the forward-looking number, and this is the most since the week of September 14 to 20, when it was 482.

### Sellers Kept Cutting, at a Slower Rate Per Contract

Price reductions were 1,891, against 1,884 last week, up 0.4%. That is 4.24 price cuts for every contract that went pending, down from 4.60 last week because contracts grew while cuts held.

### Speed Picked Up

Median days to contract was 22, against 24 on last week's re-count. The median home that closed this week took 18 days on market, and new listings came in at 2,029, up 2.4% from 1,982, or 2.57 new listings for every sale on an early count.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of October 19 to 25, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market warming slowly.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 47 out of 100, up four points from last week's reading of 43.

- **The rate slipped.** At 7.32%, down from 7.40%, the first weekly decline since late August.
- **Price edged up.** The median sold price rose 3.6% to $435,000 and homes sold for 100.0% of final list price.
- **Contracts rebounded.** Pending contracts rose 8.8% to 446, the most since the week of September 14 to 20.
- **Sellers kept cutting.** Price reductions held at 1,891, or 4.24 for every contract that went pending, down from 4.60.

If you are buying, expect more competition for the reduced homes and get your pre-approval ready. If you are selling, the data says buyers are coming back, so list into thin supply and price for this week's buyers.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Weeknight Beef and Bean Chili

Late October calls for a big pot of something warm. This is the classic: ground beef, three kinds of chili spice and two kinds of beans, simmered while you do something else. About an hour, and it is better the next day.

<img src="/images/uploads/weeknight-beef-and-bean-chili.jpg" alt="A bowl of beef and bean chili topped with shredded cheddar, sour cream and scallions, beside a skillet of cornbread on a rustic wood table with a teal linen napkin" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 8**

- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 large yellow onion, diced
- 1 green bell pepper, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 pounds ground beef
- 3 tablespoons chili powder
- 2 teaspoons ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1 1/2 teaspoons salt
- 1 can (28 ounces) crushed tomatoes
- 1 can (15 ounces) tomato sauce
- 1 cup beef broth
- 2 cans (15 ounces each) kidney beans, drained
- 1 can (15 ounces) pinto beans, drained
- Shredded cheddar, sour cream and sliced scallions for serving

1. Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven over medium heat. Cook the onion and pepper 6 to 8 minutes, until soft. Add the garlic and cook 1 minute.
2. Add the beef, breaking it up, and cook until no pink remains, about 8 minutes. Drain off excess fat if you like.
3. Stir in the chili powder, cumin, paprika, oregano and salt and cook 1 minute, until fragrant.
4. Add the crushed tomatoes, tomato sauce and broth. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer, partly covered, 30 minutes.
5. Stir in both kinds of beans and simmer another 15 minutes, until thick. Taste and add salt.
6. Serve hot with cheddar, sour cream and scallions.
