Picture a seller named Karen. She decides on a Monday to sell her Howard County home, and by the end of the week she has a photographer booked, a sign order in, and a growing list of things she wishes she had fixed last year. The listing goes live on schedule. The first buyer's inspector finds the same water stain she had been meaning to look at for two years.

Sellers who prepare early do not do more work than Karen. They do the same work in a better order, with time to make good decisions instead of fast ones.

**Quick answer:** Start preparing 3 to 4 months before you want to list, and closer to 6 months if you have big projects like a roof, HVAC, or major repairs. Work in four phases: foundation work first, cosmetic fixes 6 to 8 weeks out, staging and photography 2 to 4 weeks out, and a final readiness routine for listing week. Every phase exists to remove a decision you would otherwise have to make under pressure.

## What Should You Do 3 to 6 Months Before Listing?

This is the phase that most sellers skip, and it is the one with the highest payoff, because it is where you find out what actually matters.

One Maryland detail matters here. Sellers of most residential properties must give buyers either a disclosure statement listing the defects they actually know about, or a disclaimer statement saying the home is sold as is, before the contract is signed. Even with the disclaimer, a seller still has to disclose known latent defects. That is a reason to find problems on your own schedule and decide how to handle them, instead of learning about them mid-contract.

## What Should You Do 6 to 8 Weeks Out?

This phase is cosmetic, and it is where a home starts to look like a place a buyer can picture living in.

- **Deep clean everything.** Windows, baseboards, grout, appliances, garage. It is the cheapest improvement you can make.
- **Fix the small things.** Leaky faucets, chipped paint, loose handles, and burned-out bulbs signal deferred maintenance even when nothing is really wrong.
- **Paint high-visibility areas in a neutral color.** Entry, living areas, and any rooms with bold colors are the best places to start.
- **Improve curb appeal.** Buyers form an opinion before they open the door. See [curb appeal is not staging, it is math](/blog/curb-appeal-isnt-staging-its-math) for what pays back.
- **Depersonalize.** Pack away family photos and collections so buyers can imagine their own things there.

- **Professional staging.** Even partial staging in the living room, kitchen, and primary bedroom can help.
- **Professional photography and video.** Photos are the first showing. Buyers decide from them whether to book a tour.
- **A pre-listing inspection, if it makes sense.** It usually costs a few hundred dollars, and it means you find issues on your terms and can decide whether to fix them, price for them, or disclose them. Because you will then know about any defects, talk with your agent about how they should be handled in the disclosure paperwork.
- **Finalize your price** using current comparable sales, not the number you hoped for in the spring.

## What Should You Do During Listing Week?

By now the hard work is done. Listing week is about staying ready.

- Do a final walkthrough with your agent.
- Confirm showing logistics: lockbox, access times, pets, and notice.
- Keep the home show-ready every day, because an agent with a buyer can ask on short notice.

## What If You Do Not Have 3 to 4 Months?

Then work in order of impact:

1. Deep clean.
2. Declutter.
3. Fix structural or safety issues.
4. Improve curb appeal.

If you have to skip something, skip the finishing touches, not the foundation. A clean, decluttered, well-maintained home photographs and shows better than a half-finished renovation.

## The Bottom Line

The sellers who feel calm at listing time are the ones who made their hard decisions months earlier. Preparation does not need to be expensive. It needs to be early enough that each choice is made deliberately, and each problem is a task on your schedule instead of a surprise on someone else's.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How far in advance should I start preparing to sell my house?**

About 3 to 4 months before you want to list, and up to 6 months if you have significant repairs or big-ticket items like a roof or HVAC system to address.

**What should I fix before selling my house in Maryland?**

Start with anything structural or safety related, then the small visible items: leaks, chipped paint, loose fixtures. Ask your agent which upgrades actually move the price in your neighborhood before you spend on larger projects.

**Do I have to disclose problems when I sell in Maryland?**

Sellers of most residential properties must give buyers either a property disclosure statement of known defects or a disclaimer statement, and known latent defects still have to be disclosed. Your agent can walk you through which form applies to your sale.

**Is a pre-listing inspection worth it?**

Often, yes. It typically costs a few hundred dollars and lets you find and address issues before buyers do, which can reduce surprises and renegotiation during the contract.

**Does staging actually help?**

Many agents say so. In NAR's 2025 staging report, about half of agents reported that staging reduced time on market, and 29 percent reported higher offers of 1 to 10 percent.
