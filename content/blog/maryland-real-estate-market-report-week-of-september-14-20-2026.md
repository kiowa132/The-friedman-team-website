Denise and Walt listed their Frederick County house on Monday, September 14th.

Their agent had run the comps and suggested $525,000. Walt wanted $549,900. "We can always come down," he said, and Denise, who had sold a house once before, agreed to try it. Prices were holding. Nothing in the headlines said otherwise.

By Thursday there had been eleven showings and no offers. That same day, Freddie Mac announced the 30-year mortgage rate had jumped to 6.95%, up 19 basis points in a single week and the fourth increase in a row. On a $400,000 loan, that is about $51 more a month than the week before, and $77 more than three weeks ago. Every buyer who had toured their house was quietly running that math.

Here is what Denise and Walt could not see from their kitchen table. Across Maryland, the typical home that closed this week had taken 25 days to go under contract, up from 20 the week before. The price side of the market barely flinched: the median sold price rose 1.1% to $449,900. But buyers took a little more off the original asking price, paying 98.4% of it, down from 99.6%. Price held. Time and negotiating room were what gave.

Their agent saw it in the data, and on Friday morning made the call. Denise and Walt trusted the numbers and dropped to $529,900, still well above the first comps. Two showings came in that weekend. By Sunday night, they had one offer in hand, and a second buyer was coming back for another look on Monday.

They are not done yet, but they are in a very different place than they were on Thursday. The lesson is simple: a house is only new once, and the sellers who win this market are the ones who read the numbers and move while the window is open.

> Prices held. Time and negotiating room gave. The sellers who win this market read the numbers early and price for week one, before the window narrows.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of September 14 to 20, 2026, Maryland home prices held while the clock slowed. The median sold price rose 1.1% to $449,900, but the median time from listing to signed contract for homes that closed stretched from 20 days to 25, the biggest weekly slowdown this report has tracked. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate jumped 19 basis points to 6.95%, its fourth straight weekly increase and by far the steepest of the four. Closed sales rebounded 39.0% to 984 from the Labor Day week, though that is still 14.4% below the 1,150 closings two weeks ago. New listings dipped 4.1% to 2,269, price reductions fell 11.2% to 1,786, and pending contracts were flat at 482. The median home sold for 98.4% of its original list price, down from 99.6% the week before, while still selling for 100.0% of its final list price. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 44 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Cooling). St. Mary's County was the fastest-moving county at a 21-day average, while Baltimore City and Wicomico County carried the most new supply relative to closings. For buyers, rates are the urgent variable and there is room to negotiate. For sellers, pricing for week one matters more than it did a month ago.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### The Clock Moved More Than the Price

The median time to contract for homes that closed this week rose to 25 days from 20, a five-day jump in one week and the largest move this report has tracked since it began measuring in late August. The median sold price went the other way, up 1.1% to $449,900. Prices are holding. Speed is what changed.

### Sellers Held the Line on Price and Cut Less

Price reductions fell 11.2% to 1,786, and new listings dipped 4.1% to 2,269. The median new-listing price slipped 4.1% to $429,990, which reads as sellers listing a touch lower to start, not a market that is falling.

### Buyers Found a Little More Room

Homes that closed this week sold for a median 98.4% of their original list price, down from 99.6% a week earlier. Measured against their final list price, the median was still 100.0%, exactly where it was. The extra give came from price cuts made before the sale, not from buyers pushing accepted prices down. It is the cost of launching too high. See the Deep Dive below.

### Closings Bounced Back, but It Is a Rebound, Not a Surge

Closed sales rose 39.0% to 984 after the Labor Day week, yet that is still 14.4% below the 1,150 closings in the week before the holiday. Pending contracts were flat at 482. Total closed volume was $533.0 million.

### Mortgage Rates Rose for a Fourth Straight Week

The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed rate averaged 6.95%, up 19 basis points from 6.76% and the fourth consecutive weekly increase. On a $400,000 loan, that is about $51 more per month than the week before, and about $77 more than three weeks ago at 6.66%. The rate spike has not shown up in the closed data yet. Homes that closed this week were mostly contracts signed weeks ago, before the rate moved. The next two to three weeks of pending contracts are where it would show up if it is going to.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of September 14 to 20, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market that is cooling at the edges.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 44 out of 100, down four points from last week.

- **Price is holding.** The median sold price rose 1.1% and price reductions fell 11.2%. Sellers who priced right are not discounting.
- **Time is the pressure point.** Days to contract went from 20 to 25, and buyers are paying 98.4% of original list, down from 99.6%.
- **The rate is the wildcard.** At 6.95% and rising four weeks straight, affordability is tightening, which will eventually show up in pending contracts.

If you are buying, there is more room to negotiate and the rate is the urgent variable, so lock early. If you are selling, the data says price for week one. The typical home took 25 days to go under contract this week, and homes priced right on day one still sold at full final list price.

## Maryland's 11 Biggest Housing Markets This Week

Maryland's 11 largest counties by closings made up 83% of the state's sales this week. Here is how each one moved.

### Montgomery County Real Estate Market

The state's biggest market closed 151 homes at a $642,215 median, up 2.8% from last week. Homes took longer to sell, a 37-day average versus 25 (the median was 23, so a few long-sitting listings pulled the average up), and sold for a median 98.0% of original list. New listings fell 10.2% to 377, and the new-listing-to-closing ratio eased to 2.5x from 4.5x.

### Anne Arundel County Real Estate Market

110 closings at a $532,500 median, up 6.5%, with a 34-day average (median 13) and homes selling at 98.7% of original list. New listings dipped 5.6% to 203, a 1.8x ratio that makes it one of the tighter large markets.

### Prince George's County Real Estate Market

104 closings at a $471,995 median. That is up from $414,000, though last week's figure sat on a thin holiday-week sample of 49 closings. The average days on market improved to 33 from 37, and homes sold at a median 100.0% of original list. New listings held flat at 282, and the supply ratio dropped to 2.7x from 5.7x as closings returned.

### Baltimore County Real Estate Market

102 closings at a $381,000 median, up 2.3%, with a 27-day average (median 12), the third-fastest market in the state, and homes selling at 99.8% of original list. It also led the state in pending contracts with 88. New listings rose 10.9% to 275, a 2.7x ratio.

### Baltimore City Real Estate Market

86 closings at a $225,500 median, down 4.6%, and a 45-day average. It carries the most supply relative to closings among the large counties, 3.2x, with 276 new listings, down 5.2%. Homes sold at a median 98.7% of original list.

### Frederick County Real Estate Market

67 closings at a $527,425 median, up from $455,000. New listings fell 27.6% to 97, giving Frederick the tightest supply ratio in the state at 1.4x, and homes sold at 99.6% of original list, near the top of the large counties. The average days on market rose to 37 from 29, but the median was 20.

### Howard County Real Estate Market

51 closings at a $700,000 median, the highest of any county with a meaningful sample and up from $595,000 last week. A 26-day average made it the second-fastest market in the state. New listings fell 14.0% to 92, a 1.8x ratio, and homes sold at 98.7% of original list.

### Harford County Real Estate Market

47 closings at a $425,000 median, up 9.1%, and the pace snapped back to a 30-day average from 48 last week (median 18). New listings slipped 6.0% to 94 with 33 pending contracts, a 2.0x ratio. Homes sold at 98.3% of original list.

### Worcester County Real Estate Market

37 closings at a $464,200 median, down from $525,000, with a 66-day average (median 44). Worcester had the softest pricing among the big markets: homes sold for a median 95.8% of original list and 97.4% of final list. New listings rose 12.5% to 72, a 1.9x ratio. This is the resort market to watch as the season ends.

### Charles County Real Estate Market

36 closings at a $425,000 median, down from $479,500, with the average days on market stretching to 59 from 35 (median 41). New listings fell 18.4% to 84, a 2.3x ratio, and homes sold at 98.1% of original list.

### Carroll County Real Estate Market

29 closings at a $360,000 median, down from $449,000 last week. Medians swing with the mix of homes that close in a small week, and homes here still sold at a median 100.0% of original list, so this looks like a mix of smaller homes closing rather than a price drop. The average was 44 days (median 21). Carroll is also among the tightest markets in the state: 43 new listings, down 21.8%, a 1.5x ratio.

## The Friedman Market Momentum Index (FMMI)

## Deep Dive: The 1.6-Point Gap

**1.6 points** is the gap between what sellers first asked and what they finally got for the typical Maryland home that closed this week: 98.4% of the original list price, versus 100.0% of the final list price. Last week that gap was 0.4 points. It quadrupled in seven days.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Apple Cider Pork Chops

Maryland apple season is in full swing, from the orchards in Carroll and Frederick counties to the farm stands along the Shore. This is a one-skillet dinner that leans on exactly what is in season, and it comes together in about 35 minutes.

<img src="/images/uploads/maryland-real-estate-market-report-week-of-september-14-20-2026-apple-cider-pork-chops.jpg" alt="Cast-iron skillet of seared bone-in pork chops with caramelized apples, onions, and thyme in an apple cider pan sauce" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 4**

- 4 bone-in pork chops, about 1 inch thick
- 1 teaspoon salt and 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, sliced
- 2 firm apples (Honeycrisp or Stayman Winesap), cored and cut into 8 wedges each
- 1 cup unfiltered apple cider
- 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1 teaspoon dried)
- 1 tablespoon butter

1. Pat the chops dry and season both sides with the salt and pepper.
2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Sear the chops about
   4 minutes per side until golden, then move them to a plate.
3. Lower the heat to medium. Cook the onion for 4 minutes, then add the apples
   and cook 3 minutes more.
4. Pour in the cider, mustard, vinegar, and thyme, scraping up the browned
   bits, and simmer 3 minutes.
5. Nestle the chops back in, cover, and cook on low 8 to 10 minutes, until the
   center reaches 145 degrees F. Let them rest 3 minutes.
6. Stir the butter into the pan sauce and spoon the apples, onions, and sauce
   over the chops.

Serve with mashed potatoes or roasted sweet potatoes and a bitter green salad
to balance the sweetness.
