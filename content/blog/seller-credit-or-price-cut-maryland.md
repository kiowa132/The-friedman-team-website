Two offers come in on the same $400,000 house. The first asks for $10,000 off the price. The second pays full price and asks the seller for a $10,000 credit at closing. Most sellers look at those two offers and see the same deal. For the buyer, they are not close.

**Quick answer:** a $10,000 price cut and a $10,000 seller credit cost a seller roughly the same amount. But with the 30-year rate at 7.11%, a credit used to buy down the buyer's interest rate saves the buyer about $127 a month on a $400,000 home with 5% down, while the same $10,000 as a price cut saves about $64. The sale also records at full price, which protects the seller's comps. Credits are capped by loan type, usually 3% to 6% of the price.

## Why this matters in Maryland right now

Maryland sellers filed 1,620 price reductions in the week of November 9 to 15, 2026, according to The Friedman Team's weekly Bright MLS pull, and the Freddie Mac 30-year fixed rate averaged 7.11%. Buyers are still out there, but the monthly payment stops more deals than the sticker price does.

A price cut lowers the price. A seller credit can lower the rate. At 7%, the rate is where the money is.

Assumptions: 5% down, 30-year fixed at 7.11%, principal and interest only.

- **No concession:** a $380,000 loan at 7.11% is about $2,556 a month.
- **$10,000 price cut:** the price drops to $390,000 and the loan to $370,500, about $2,492 a month.
- **$10,000 seller credit:** the price stays at $400,000. About $7,600 of the credit buys two discount points (one point costs 1% of the loan and typically lowers the rate by around a quarter point), taking the rate to roughly 6.61%. That is about $2,429 a month, and the other $2,400 goes toward closing costs.

<div style="background:#FAF8F5;border-left:4px solid #C9A96A;padding:16px 20px;margin:24px 0;border-radius:6px;">Over five years, the gap between the price cut and the credit is almost $3,800 in the buyer's pocket, from the same $10,000 of the seller's money. Lenders price points differently day to day, so the buyer's Loan Estimate is the real number. The direction does not change.</div>

## What about a 2-1 buydown?

Some buyers want the biggest break up front. A 2-1 temporary buydown uses the seller's credit to lower the rate by 2% in year one and 1% in year two before it returns to the note rate. On the same $380,000 loan at 7.11%, the payment is about $2,066 in year one, about $2,305 in year two, and about $2,556 after that. Funding it costs roughly $8,900, which fits inside a $10,000 credit. It suits buyers who expect their income to rise or plan to refinance if rates fall.

## Why sellers should offer a credit before cutting the price

**Your list price history stays clean.** Every price cut shows up in the listing history that buyers and agents see. A credit is a closing detail.

**Your sale records at full price.** A $400,000 sale with a credit records as $400,000, which supports your appraisal and the comps your neighbors will be measured against.

**It actually moves buyers.** A smaller price is easy to forget. A payment that is $127 lower every month is a reason to write the offer. ## How much can a seller contribute in Maryland?

Seller credits are limited by the buyer's loan, not by Maryland law. On a conventional loan, Fannie Mae generally caps interested party contributions at 3% of the price when the buyer puts down less than 10%, and 6% with 10% to 25% down. FHA and VA loans have their own limits. The buyer's lender confirms the exact number before the contract is signed.

## What buyers should ask before writing an offer

1. Ask your lender: "If the seller gives me a $10,000 credit, what does that do to my payment?"
2. Write the offer around the payment you want, not only the price.
3. Know your loan's credit cap so the credit you ask for can actually be used.
4. Keep the rest of the offer clean: a strong pre-approval and a reasonable inspection window make a credit easier for a seller to say yes to.

## The Bottom Line

A $10,000 price cut and a $10,000 seller credit cost a seller about the same. With rates above 7%, the credit is usually worth more to the buyer and protects the seller's sale price. Sellers should offer it before they cut. Buyers should ask for it before they ask for a lower price.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a seller credit better than a price reduction?**
Often, yes, when rates are high. A credit used for discount points lowers the buyer's rate and monthly payment more than the same dollars taken off the price, and the sale still records at the full price.

**How much can a seller credit a buyer in Maryland?**
It depends on the loan. Conventional loans generally allow 3% of the price with less than 10% down and 6% with 10% to 25% down. FHA and VA loans have their own caps, and the lender confirms the limit.

**How much does a discount point lower a mortgage rate?**
One point costs 1% of the loan amount and typically lowers the rate by about a quarter of a percentage point, though it varies by lender and day.

**What is a 2-1 buydown?**
A temporary buydown that lowers the rate 2% in the first year and 1% in the second, funded upfront, often by a seller credit. After year two the payment returns to the note rate.

**Does a seller credit affect the appraisal?**
The home still has to appraise at the contract price. A credit does not lower the recorded price, which is why it helps protect comps, but the price itself still has to be supported by the market.
