Tasha and Eric had the paperwork half filled out when their agent called on Monday. They wanted to list their Bowie colonial at $479,900, a number they had been carrying around since spring.

Their agent, Nicole, had spent that morning with the weekend's Bright MLS numbers. "Prince George's County had 280 new listings last week and 68 closings," she told them. "That is four new homes for every one that sold. The median sold price here was $446,000. At $479,900 you would be the most expensive house on the street the first weekend, and the first weekend is when the showings happen."

Eric did the math out loud. A rate of 7.40% was not going to help any buyer stretch. Tasha asked the question Nicole had hoped for: "So what do we do differently?" Nicole did not tell them to give anything away. She walked them through three comparable sales within a mile and showed them where the pricing cliffs were. They settled on $459,900, a number that looked fair next to the closed sales and still left room to negotiate.

The house went live Thursday with new photos and a clean disclosure packet. Nicole kept the first weekend open for showings and told them to expect a quiet phone until Saturday.

By Sunday night there were nine showings on the calendar and two offers in the inbox. One was at full price, with a seven-day inspection window and a settlement date that worked around their move. They signed it Monday morning.

They did not get the spring number. They got a signed contract in four days, in a county where homes can sit for weeks, because their agent read the data before the listing went live and priced for the first weekend.

> In the counties where supply is running at three or four times sales, the right price on the first weekend decides who sets the terms.

## Quick Answer: What the Maryland Housing Market Did This Week

For the week of October 5 to 11, 2026, Maryland closings fell back from the quarter-end rush while prices held. The median sold price was $440,000, up 2.3% from $430,000 on a like-for-like re-pull, and price reductions held flat at 1,836. Closed sales reached an early count of 763, below the first counts of each of the last three weeks, and new listings were 2,126, which is 2.79 new listings for every sale, up from 1.68 last week. Pending contracts rose 6.1% to 433, the first increase after two weekly declines. The median time from listing to signed contract for homes that closed held at 23 days. The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 7.40%, up 12 basis points, its seventh straight weekly increase and the highest reading this report has tracked. The Friedman Market Momentum Index reads 42 out of 100 (Balanced Market, Catching Its Breath), down three points. Carroll County was the fastest-moving large county at a 17-day average, while Prince George's County, Frederick County and Baltimore City carried the most new supply relative to closings. For buyers, the county you are in decides how much room you have to negotiate. For sellers, the first weekend is your whole market.

## What This Week's Numbers Mean for Maryland Buyers and Sellers

### Closings Came Back Down

763 Maryland homes have closed so far this week, against 1,485 for the quarter-end week when re-counted today. Total closed volume was about $418.2 million, down from $768.5 million. Late-entered closings keep posting for days after a week ends (last week's count climbed from 1,304 to 1,485 after our first pull), so expect this number to drift up. It still came in below the first counts of each of the last three weeks: 984, 933 and 1,304.

### Price Held Its Ground

The median sold price was $440,000, up 2.3% from last week's $430,000 on a like-for-like re-pull. Homes that closed sold for a median 98.7% of their original list price and 100.0% of final list price. Price reductions were essentially flat at 1,836, against 1,843 last week.

### Speed Held Steady

Median days to contract was 23, the same as last week's re-count of 23. The median home that closed this week took 20 days on market.

### Supply Outran Sales

2,126 new listings against 763 early closings is 2.79 new listings for every sale, up from 1.68 last week and 2.18 the week before, measured the same way on first counts. Some of that is the quarter-end hangover and the early count, but it is the widest gap in the last four weeks.

### Contracts Ticked Up

Pending contracts rose 6.1% to 433, the first increase after two straight weekly declines. Pending is the forward-looking number, and this is the first sign in three weeks that buyers are signing again.

### The Rate Set Another High

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 7.40%, up from 7.28%, the seventh consecutive weekly increase. On a $400,000 loan, the move from last week alone adds roughly $33 a month; measured against the 6.66% low this report tracked in late August, it is closer to $199 a month more.

## Is Maryland a Buyer's or Seller's Market Right Now?

As of the week of October 5 to 11, 2026, Maryland is a **balanced market catching its breath.** The Friedman Market Momentum Index sits at 42 out of 100, down three points from last week's reading of 45.

- **Closings cooled.** An early count of 763 closed sales, after the quarter-end rush.
- **Price held.** The median sold price rose 2.3% to $440,000 and price reductions held flat at 1,836.
- **Contracts turned up.** Pending contracts rose 6.1% to 433, the first gain in three weeks.
- **The rate keeps climbing.** At 7.40% and rising seven weeks straight, affordability pressure is real and building.

If you are buying, know your county before you decide how hard to push: in the loose counties you have room to negotiate, and in the tight ones a clean offer on day one wins. If you are selling, the data says the first weekend is your whole market, so price for this week's buyers.

## Bonus From My Kitchen: Classic Sunday Pot Roast

The first real chill of fall calls for a pot roast. Sear it, put it in the oven, and let it do the work. About 3 1/2 hours, almost all of it hands-off, and the house smells great the whole time.

<img src="/images/uploads/classic-sunday-pot-roast.jpg" alt="Platter of sliced pot roast with carrots, potatoes and fresh thyme and gravy, on a rustic wood table with a teal linen napkin" style="width:100%;height:auto;margin:20px 0;border-radius:6px;" />

**Serves 6**

- 1 beef chuck roast (3 1/2 to 4 pounds)
- 1 1/2 teaspoons salt, plus more to taste
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
- 1 large yellow onion, sliced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 2 cups beef broth
- 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 bay leaves
- 4 large carrots, peeled and cut into 2-inch chunks
- 1 1/2 pounds small Yukon Gold potatoes, halved
- 1 tablespoon cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water (optional, for the gravy)

1. Heat the oven to 325 degrees. Pat the roast dry and season all over with the salt and pepper.
2. Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Sear the roast 4 to 5 minutes per side, until deeply browned, then move it to a plate.
3. Add the onion to the pot and cook 5 minutes. Stir in the garlic and tomato paste and cook 1 minute. Pour in the broth and Worcestershire, scraping up the browned bits.
4. Return the roast to the pot, tuck in the thyme and bay leaves, cover, and cook in the oven 2 hours.
5. Add the carrots and potatoes around the roast, cover, and cook 1 hour more, until the meat shreds easily with a fork and the vegetables are tender.
6. Rest the roast 10 minutes. Remove the thyme and bay leaves and skim the fat from the juices. For a thicker gravy, stir in the cornstarch mixture and simmer on the stovetop 2 minutes. Slice or shred the roast and serve with the vegetables and gravy.
